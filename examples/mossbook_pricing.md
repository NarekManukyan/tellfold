# Mossbook is changing how it's priced. Here's what that means for you.

*Posted by Ines Okafor-Brandt, co-founder of Mossbook Labs, on the Mossbook blog. Fictional document written as an example seed for Tellfold.*

Six years ago, two of us built Mossbook in a rented garage because our own houseplants kept dying. The idea was simple: take a photo of a plant, get a care plan, get reminded when it needs water, light or a new pot. We sold it for a one-time price of $24.99, and about 410,000 of you bought it. Thank you. That one purchase has paid for six years of updates, a plant-identification model that now knows more than 9,000 species, and a team that grew from two people to nineteen.

Today we're announcing the biggest change we have ever made to Mossbook, and we want to explain it plainly.

## What is changing

Starting **March 3**, new users will download Mossbook for free and can track up to five plants without paying. To go beyond five plants, or to use the features listed below, they'll need **Mossbook Plus**, which costs **$3.99 a month or $29.99 a year**.

Mossbook Plus includes:

- Unlimited plants and rooms
- Photo diagnosis ("why are the leaves on my fiddle-leaf fig turning brown?"), which runs on our servers
- Weather-aware watering for balcony and garden plants
- Household sharing for up to six people
- The new Grow Journal with time-lapse photos

## What happens to people who already paid

This is the part we argued about the most, so here it is in detail.

If you bought Mossbook at any point before March 3, you keep everything you have today, forever. Unlimited plants, reminders, the species library, offline mode. None of it will move behind the subscription.

On top of that, every existing buyer gets **one full year of Mossbook Plus for free**, starting March 3. After that year you can subscribe at a lifetime discount of $17.99 a year, or simply keep using the version you paid for.

Two features will be Plus-only for everyone after the free year ends: photo diagnosis and weather-aware watering. Both of these cost us real money every time you use them. Photo diagnosis alone runs about 2.1 million image checks a month on rented GPUs, and the weather data comes from a commercial provider that bills us per request. Last year these two features cost more than the app earned from new sales.

## Why we're doing this

The honest answer is that one-time pricing worked when Mossbook was an app that lived entirely on your phone. It no longer is. The features people tell us they love most are the ones that run on servers, and servers bill us every month whether or not anyone buys the app that month.

We looked at alternatives. We could have added ads, but nobody wants a banner for fertilizer spikes between them and their monstera. We could have sold your plant data to garden retailers, and we will never do that. We could have kept prices as they are and slowly stopped improving the app, which is what happens to a lot of one-time-purchase apps. We think a small subscription is the most honest of those options.

## What we're committing to

We know a pricing change asks you to trust us, so here are commitments you can hold us to:

1. **Your data stays yours.** You can export all of your plants, photos and journal entries as a ZIP file at any time, on any plan, including the free one.
2. **Offline mode stays free.** Reminders and care plans will always work without an internet connection and without a subscription.
3. **No price increase for current subscribers before 2028.** If we ever raise prices, anyone already subscribed keeps their price.
4. **Student and community-garden discount.** Verified students and registered community gardens get Plus for $9.99 a year.
5. **We'll publish our numbers.** Once a quarter we will post how many people pay for Plus and what we spent on servers, so you can see where the money goes.

## What's next

The update with the new plans goes out on March 3 for iOS and Android. The free year for existing buyers will be applied automatically; you don't need to do anything. If it doesn't show up, write to support and a human will fix it within a day.

We're also hosting an open Q&A on our community forum on February 24, where the whole team will answer questions for as long as people have them. Bring the hard ones.

We know some of you will be disappointed. Some of you bought Mossbook precisely because it wasn't a subscription, and we respect that. We've tried to make sure that nobody who already paid loses anything they paid for. If we got something wrong, tell us. We've changed our plans because of your feedback before, and we'll do it again.

Thank you for six years of dead-plant rescues, very blurry leaf photos, and kind emails.

Ines and the Mossbook team
