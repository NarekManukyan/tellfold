# Consultation: making Market Street car-free

*Published by Fenwick Town Council's transport committee for public consultation. Fictional document written as an example seed for Tellfold.*

## The proposal

From **1 May next year**, the 400-metre stretch of Market Street between the Clock Tower and Bridge Road would close to private cars between 10 am and 7 pm, every day of the week. The council would:

- widen the pavements and add 60 trees, benches and space for café seating;
- keep access for buses, taxis, bicycles, emergency vehicles and blue-badge holders at all times;
- allow deliveries before 10 am and after 7 pm only;
- remove the 48 on-street parking spaces, and reduce the price of the Riverside car park (5 minutes' walk away, 320 spaces) from €2.50 to €1.50 an hour for the first two hours;
- run a free electric shuttle every 12 minutes between the train station, the Riverside car park and the Clock Tower.

The scheme would cost €3.1 million, of which €2.4 million comes from a national active-travel grant that must be spent by the end of next year.

## Why

Market Street carries about 9,000 cars a day, but traffic counts show that around 60% are driving through, not stopping. Air quality on the street broke the legal limit for nitrogen dioxide on 31 days last year. Footfall in the town centre has fallen 18% in five years. Towns of a similar size that pedestrianised their main street report footfall rising 10% to 30% within two years, although some shops in those towns closed during the building works.

## What we have heard so far

In early conversations, some shop owners told us they worry that customers who drive will go to the out-of-town retail park instead. Disability groups asked about drop-off points. Residents of the side streets asked whether traffic will move onto their roads. The council has commissioned a traffic study and will publish it before a final vote.

## Timeline

- Consultation open now, closes 30 November.
- Final council vote: 14 January.
- Works, if approved: February to April, carried out at night where possible.

You can respond online, at the library, or at the drop-in sessions at the town hall on 12 and 19 November.
