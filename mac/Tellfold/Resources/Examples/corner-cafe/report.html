Tellfold simulation report

Simulated regulars mostly rally behind price rise; a quiet fringe stays at risk

Question: How will the café's regulars and neighbours react to the new prices and the neighbour card over the first weeks, and will the café lose customers?

Across six rounds the simulated crowd moved from early unease (r1 sentiment -0.06) to strong, stable support (+0.73 to +0.75 by r5-r6), driven by the café's transparent cost math and the neighbour card (@hanna_lindenrye #10, #19). A hostile food-blog label in round 3 ('greedy') actually backfired, pushing sentiment from +0.34 before the event to +0.57 after it as regulars like @rosalind_teamlindenr #21 and @danilo_bakes_back #26 rallied to defend the owners. Two former skeptics, @sandra_alderave and @joon_flatwhite_daily, were won over once concrete savings numbers appeared (#17, #18), suggesting most paying regulars stay. But @gerald_onalder's pension-affordability thread #22 drew five replies and no resolution, and several budget-constrained or silent personas (@margit_speaksup, @mairead_otf, @kiano_brokestudent) never moved off a critical or unposted stance, flagging a minority genuinely at risk of reducing visits. The real behavioral test — the 26 Feb open evening and the 2 March price rise itself — falls outside the simulated rounds, so the crowd's optimism is about discourse, not yet about actual foot traffic.

Prediction

Medium confidence

The single most likely outcome is that Linden & Rye retains the large majority of its regular customer base and does not see meaningful customer loss in the first weeks, because the dominant simulated camps (café team, long-time supporters, and persuaded skeptics like @sandra_alderave and @joon_flatwhite_daily) converged on viewing the price rise plus neighbour card as fair and well-explained (#10, #17, #19, #25), and a hostile press label failed to dent sentiment (round-3 injected event). This depends on the café actually delivering the promised open evening and following through on listening to concerns, as Hanna, Tomás and the official account repeatedly promise (#24, #27, #29, #31). The strongest counter-signal is @gerald_onalder's unresolved pension-affordability thread #22 — five replies of reassurance but no actual solution — plus several budget-squeezed or silent personas (a05, a08, a16) whose stance never shifted, implying a smaller but real segment of fixed-income regulars could quietly cut back regardless of the overall positive mood.

Analysis

Round-by-round evolution

The simulation opens with the café's own announcements (#1, #2) and supportive first reactions, giving r0 a sentiment of +0.43. r1 dips sharply to -0.06 as outside voices like @sandra_alderave #7 and @joon_flatwhite_daily #6 push back on specific price jumps and the new Tuesday closure.

  • r2 rebounds strongly to +0.73 once @hanna_lindenrye lays out concrete savings math in #10 ('€12/month saves regulars €30+'), converting doubters such as Sandra (#17) and Joon (#18).
  • r3 dips slightly to +0.48 right as the injected food-blog 'greedy' story lands, but the dip is shallow and short-lived.
  • r4-r6 climb steadily to +0.67, +0.73 and finally +0.75, the highest of the run, as the team keeps directly answering a lone critic (@gerald_onalder) in a long public thread (#24, #27, #28, #29, #30).

Activity cools from 6-7 new posts/round early on to 2-3 by r5-r6, with likes becoming the dominant action (10 likes in r4), suggesting the conversation settled into quiet agreement rather than continued debate.

Camps and who leads them

Café team driving outreach (a01 @lindenandrye, a02 @tomas_lindenrye, a03 @hanna_lindenrye, a04 @lindenandrye_ig): the most-followed and most active voices, repeatedly offering cost transparency (#19) and personally answering critics (#24, #29, #30, #31).

Firmly won-over regulars (a09 @danilo_bakes_back, a12 @ruben_ratesit, a13 @bea_sourdoughsunday, a19 @rosalind_teamlindenr): never wavered, framing the rise as fairness to staff and bakers rather than greed (#21, #25, #26), and leading the pushback against the food-blog story.

Persuaded by the numbers (a15 @sandra_alderave, a17 @joon_flatwhite_daily): started skeptical or annoyed (#6, #7) but flipped to support once shown concrete monthly savings (#17, #18).

Sympathetic but worried (a14 @desmond_midtown, a18 @henrik_onthecorner): understand the economics but keep voicing concern for customers who might be 'left behind' (#5, #9), without ever becoming hostile.

Critical and disengaged fringe (a06 @gerald_onalder, a08 @margit_speaksup, a05 @mairead_otf, a16 @kiano_brokestudent, plus non-posting a07, a10, a11, a20): Gerald is the only one who actually posts a sustained critique (#22, mean sentiment -0.60); the others share a critical or indifferent seed stance but generated no scored posts, so their real reaction is unmeasured.

Turning points, including the injected event

The clearest structural turning point is @hanna_lindenrye's cost-breakdown reply #10 in r1, which supplied the specific '€30+/month' savings figure that later convinces both Sandra (#17) and Joon (#18) — this single post anchors the shift from r1's -0.06 to r2's +0.73.

The injected event — a local food blog calling the prices 'greedy' at round 3 — had a measured positive effect on the simulated crowd rather than the intended negative one: sentiment rose from +0.34 before the event to +0.57 from that round on, even as posting volume fell from 6.0 to 4.0 posts/round. Hanna's direct rebuttal #19 ('Flour is up 40%... If that's greedy, what's fair?') and Rosalind's #21 ('Thirty years on this street - I know greed. This is integrity.') are the posts that visibly absorbed the attack and turned it into a rallying moment.

A second, slower-burn turning point is the Gerald thread (#22, r3), which by r6 has drawn five separate responses from every café-side voice (#24, #27, #28, #29, #30) — showing the owners publicly prioritizing one persistent critic over broad declarations, which likely helped sustain the late-round sentiment plateau around +0.73-0.75.

Dissenting views worth watching

@gerald_onalder's thread #22 is the single most durable dissent in the dataset: a pensioner-coded voice asking 'how long before daily regulars on pensions just can't afford to visit?' It generated five replies but, as of r6, no concrete fix beyond repeated invitations to the open evening (#30) — Hanna's own private note flags this as 'quiet worry... more than blog noise.'

Several seeded critical personas never produced scored posts at all (a05 @mairead_otf, a08 @margit_speaksup, a16 @kiano_brokestudent), meaning their stated budget-driven skepticism is present in the seed documents but invisible in the actual conversation — a silent minority whose real-world analogue could still reduce visits without ever complaining publicly.

@henrik_onthecorner's single post #9 ('when every shop on the street goes up at once, some people get left behind') is notable as a small-business-owner perspective that never resolves into either full support or opposition, remaining a watch-point for whether broader neighbourhood price pressure (not just this café) drives attrition.

Risks and caveats

What could prove this wrong

  • Real customers may be far more price-sensitive than agents, since all personas share one underlying model and can converge toward consensus.
  • The decisive real-world events — the 26 Feb open evening and the 2 March price rise — occur at or after the simulated window's edge, so actual behavior change is untested.
  • Fixed-income regulars like the Gerald persona may quietly reduce visits in reality without ever posting publicly, unlike in this discourse-based simulation.
  • Neighbour card uptake requires an upfront €12 commitment that may not track the enthusiasm shown in posts and likes.

Limits of this simulation

  • Scale: 20 simulated agents over 6 rounds produced 31 posts; agent decisions came from the 'haiku' model. Treat this as a scenario exploration, not a poll or a measurement of real people.
  • Only 20 agents were simulated, and 5 of them (a01-a04, a19) are café-affiliated or long-tenure voices, which can skew the discourse toward supportive framing.
  • All personas run on one underlying language model, risking convergence of opinions rather than genuine real-world heterogeneity.
  • Six compressed rounds stand in for 'the first weeks' after 2 March; the actual price-rise period and open evening outcome are not captured.
  • Sentiment scores are model-assigned, not validated against real purchasing or attendance data.
  • 8 of 20 agents (a05, a07, a08, a10, a11, a16, a20) produced no scored posts, so their true reaction is inferred only from seed stance, not observed behavior.
