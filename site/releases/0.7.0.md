# Tellfold 0.7.0

- **A crowd of the people it affects.** Before writing the cast, the planner names the groups the announcement touches and what it changes for each, and how they're likely to lean at first. The people are spread over those groups and start from that mix, with at least 15% on each side so there's always someone to argue with. The run's `config.json` records the groups, the mix and the planner's reason.
- **Organisations stick to the facts.** Company, council and official accounts in the crowd can explain and defend what was announced, but they no longer announce discounts, exemptions, reviews or dates it never had. Their posts are checked against your documents, and a post that invents something is dropped. The run keeps a record of what was dropped.
- **Everyone has their say early.** Every person posts in the first rounds, and the crowd stays on your announcement instead of drifting to other news. People who are undecided weigh both sides instead of only complaining, and someone who's made their point mostly goes quiet instead of getting angrier every round.
- **The answer starts from the first reaction.** Tellfold counts how each person first reacted, for, against or neither, and the report leads with that. A report now also has a section listing every objection anyone raised, so the one or two people who spotted a real problem aren't lost behind the loudest voices.
- **A truer cost line.** The estimate on the New Simulation sheet counts the extra planner calls a run now makes.
- **How accurate is it?** We tested Tellfold against 43 real announcements whose outcome is known, and published everything in [ACCURACY.md](https://github.com/NarekManukyan/tellfold/blob/main/docs/ACCURACY.md). Short version: the simulated crowd is useful for rehearsing the arguments people will raise, but it doesn't predict the overall direction of a reaction better than asking the same AI model directly, and it rarely calls good news good. Treat a run as a rehearsal, not a forecast.
- Runs you made before don't change, and new versions of an old run follow the rules it was made with.
