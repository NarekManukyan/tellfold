# Outcome: UT Austin ends new enrolment in ethnic and gender studies majors

## Timeline

- 2026-09-15 — The interim chair of the Department of Social and Cultural Analysis tells faculty the provost's office has rejected the department's plan to keep its majors ([KUT, 16 Sep 2026](https://www.kut.org/education/2026-09-16/university-of-texas-austin-cuts-ethnic-gender-studies-majors-dei)).
- 2026-09-16 — KUT reports the cuts; a university spokesperson confirms no new enrolment and that current students can finish. Faculty call the decision censorship, say leadership communication has been unusually unclear, and warn of falling course enrolment and possible job losses. European and Eurasian Studies faculty say they were not told their majors were being merged ([KUT, 16 Sep 2026](https://www.kut.org/education/2026-09-16/university-of-texas-austin-cuts-ethnic-gender-studies-majors-dei)).
- 2026-09-17 — The Texas Tribune reports the interim chair's follow-up email saying the decision was made without public knowledge and apparently without the dean's knowledge. The leader of the campus AAUP chapter calls it ideological pressure that tells students these fields have no standalone value. The university does not respond to requests for comment ([Texas Tribune, 17 Sep 2026](https://www.texastribune.org/2026/09/17/university-texas-austin-cuts-race-gender-majors/)).
- 2026-09-22 — A conservative higher-education outlet welcomes the cuts as overdue stewardship ([Legal Insurrection, 22 Sep 2026](https://legalinsurrection.com/2026/09/u-texas-at-austin-drops-four-majors-focused-on-race-gender/)).
- 2026-09-25 — About 50 people, organised by the Texas State Employees Union, march near campus against the changes, framing them as an attack on academic freedom; students say they have little information about what comes next ([Austin Chronicle, 1 Oct 2026](https://www.austinchronicle.com/news/ut-workers-and-students-march-against-changes-to-academic-programs/)).

## Overall reaction

Negative among faculty and the students and staff who spoke publicly, with a clear political split outside campus: conservative commentators welcomed the move. Campus protest was modest in size (about 50 at the main march). The strongest themes were lack of consultation and communication, and a belief that the decision was political rather than academic.

## What people objected to

- It is censorship and an attack on academic freedom.
- It was imposed without consulting faculty; even the dean apparently did not know, and some departments learned from the press.
- It is driven by state and federal political pressure, not student demand.
- It narrows students' learning and career options and devalues these fields.
- It will cut course enrolment and could lead to job losses for faculty.

## Who pushed back

- Faculty in the Department of Social and Cultural Analysis and in European and Eurasian Studies.
- The campus chapter of the American Association of University Professors.
- The Texas State Employees Union, with some students and staff.

Supporters of the decision included conservative commentators.

## Did they back down?

No. The university kept the decision and did not publicly explain why it rejected the faculty proposal. Faculty were left to propose a replacement major by the end of the semester.
