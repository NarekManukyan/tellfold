# UT Austin stops new enrolment in three ethnic and gender studies majors

## Who the body is

The University of Texas at Austin is the flagship public university of the University of Texas System, with a large College of Liberal Arts. In February 2026 the university announced it would consolidate seven ethnic and gender studies departments in the college into two new departments, saying the college had become overly fragmented and needed more intellectual coherence. One of the new units is the Department of Social and Cultural Analysis, which houses six majors, nine minors and certificates and four graduate programmes, including African and African Diaspora Studies, Mexican American and Latina/o Studies, Women's and Gender Studies, American Studies and Urban Studies. Officials said at the time that most majors could be consolidated before fall 2027.

Under a 2025 state law and a May 2026 Board of Regents policy, UT System campus presidents have broad power to close academic programmes on grounds such as cost, demand or fit with the university's mission. In August 2026 faculty in the new department were given two weeks to propose which majors it should offer; they proposed a new combined social and cultural analysis major while keeping most existing majors.

## What was announced

On Tuesday 15 September 2026 the department's interim chair told faculty by email that the provost's office had rejected the department's proposal to keep its majors. On 16 September a university spokesperson confirmed that:

- UT Austin is no longer enrolling new students in the African and African Diaspora Studies, Mexican American and Latina/o Studies, and Women's and Gender Studies majors.
- Students already enrolled in those majors can finish their degrees.
- American Studies and Urban Studies remain as majors.
- Faculty may propose a new major for the department by the end of the semester.

Draft curriculum documents also show plans to merge four majors in the Department of European and Eurasian Studies into two.

## Stated reasons

The university has pointed to student demand, the size and scope of the departments, its academic mission and the distribution of resources, and earlier described the consolidation as a way to reduce fragmentation. It did not give a separate explanation for rejecting the department's proposal.

## How it was communicated

Internally, through the interim chair's email to faculty; publicly, through a short spokesperson statement after news organisations reported the email. The university website still listed the degrees on the day.
