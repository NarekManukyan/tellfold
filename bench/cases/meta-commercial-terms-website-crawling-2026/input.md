# Meta updates its Commercial Terms and Self-Serve Ad Terms, effective 30 October 2026

*Benchmark seed: a neutral summary of the announcement as it stood on 2026-10-15, written for the Tellfold accuracy test. Not the original text. (Meta notified business users in early October 2026; the exact day of the notice is not confirmed in sources.)*

## Who is announcing

Meta runs Facebook, Instagram, WhatsApp and Threads. Millions of businesses, including retailers, brands, small firms, news publishers and creators, use Meta's business products: Facebook and Instagram Pages, ads, product catalogues, Meta Business Suite and WhatsApp Business. Using them requires accepting Meta's Commercial Terms, plus supplementary terms such as the Self-Serve Ad Terms. The Commercial Terms in force date from March 2024. Since May 2024, Meta's generative-AI ad terms have allowed it to crawl URLs that advertisers submit, for ad purposes.

## What was announced

In early October 2026 Meta told business users, through a blog post and notices in Meta Business Suite, that revised **Commercial Terms** and **Self-Serve Ad Terms** take effect on **30 October 2026**. The main changes:

- **New crawling permission (Section 1, "Licences and rights you provide Meta").** Businesses authorise Meta, or others acting for it, to use automated software to access, index, cache, collect or crawl domains and web pages "associated with" the business's products or services, or made available through its use of Meta products. Meta may create test credentials to access those pages and fetch updates periodically. The content may be used to provide, develop, improve, research and train Meta's products.
- **New section on AI features**, plus updated warranty, liability and indemnity wording, and five supplementary term sets brought in by reference.
- **Acceptance.** Continuing to use Meta's business products after 30 October counts as agreeing. There is no separate opt-out for the crawling paragraph. A business that does not agree can stop using Meta's business products.

**Stated reasons:** Meta says crawling language was previously spread across several agreements, including its generative-AI ad terms, and the update brings it together in one place. It says the purpose is to pull accurate information about products sold on third-party websites, for ads and content recommendations. Meta says the update does not change how it crawls the web, and that site owners can still manage crawler access with robots.txt.

## How it was communicated

A Meta business blog post, a Business Help Center notice and in-product notices in Meta Business Suite in early October 2026, with a preview of the new terms on Meta's legal site.
