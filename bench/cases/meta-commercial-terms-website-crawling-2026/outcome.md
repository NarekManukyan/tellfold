# Outcome: Meta's Commercial Terms crawling clause (notified early October 2026)

## Timeline

- Early October 2026 — Meta notifies business users of revised terms effective 30 October ([Nieman Lab](https://www.niemanlab.org/2026/10/metas-new-commercial-terms-will-allow-the)). Exact date not confirmed.
- 2026-10-15 — Nieman Lab reports that legal experts (Matt Topic, James Grimmelmann, Benjamin Sobel) read the clause as allowing Meta to scrape news sites for AI training. A small-business owner who tipped them off calls the move "arrogant" and considers closing his accounts ([Nieman Lab](https://www.niemanlab.org/2026/10/metas-new-commercial-terms-will-allow-the)).
- 2026-10-16 — Meta's Andy Stone says the reports are false and the update is about product data, not news sites. The News/Media Alliance's Danielle Coffey calls the terms "outrageous and probably illegal". The European Publishers Council writes to the European Commission ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/metas-new-terms-let-it-crawl-business-websites-to-train-ai-from-oct-30/); [Unite.AI](https://www.unite.ai/?p=488397); [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/metas-new-terms-expand-crawling-rights-beyond-advertisers/)).
- 2026-10-17 — Meta quietly removes the sentence allowing crawled content to be used to "train" its products. Experts say the remaining crawling permission is still broad ([Unite.AI](https://www.unite.ai/?p=488397); [Nieman Lab sidebar](https://www.niemanlab.org/2026/10/metas-new-commercial-terms-will-allow-the)).
- 2026-10-21/22 — UK politicians and German publishers urge regulators to pause the terms, and publishers press Meta to drop them ([Nieman Lab sidebar](https://www.niemanlab.org/2026/10/metas-new-commercial-terms-will-allow-the)).
- 2026-10-23 — Meta tells Reuters the terms will now take effect on 20 November, wording otherwise unchanged. The European Commission welcomes the delay. The UK News Media Association calls it "a pause not a solution" ([Relevant Audience](https://www.relevantaudience.com/meta/meta-commercial-terms-delayed-to-november-20-2026/)).
- 2026-10-27/28 — US Senator Adam Schiff tells Meta to scrap the terms, and Meta pledges to consult publishers before finalising them ([Nieman Lab sidebar](https://www.niemanlab.org/2026/10/metas-new-commercial-terms-will-allow-the)).
- Late Oct / early Nov 2026 — A Norwegian court grants a temporary injunction suspending the new terms, in a case backed by 180 Norwegian media companies. The UK CMA says it will investigate ([404 Media](https://404media.co/meta-generative-ai-commercial-terms-on-pause)).
- Week of 2026-11-02 — Meta pauses the update entirely: the new terms will not take effect on 20 November, and the existing terms stay in force ([404 Media](https://404media.co/meta-generative-ai-commercial-terms-on-pause)).

## Overall reaction

Strongly negative from news publishers, their trade bodies, legal scholars and some small businesses. Pressure grew within days to politicians and regulators in the EU, UK, Norway and the US. Meta's argument that this was a technical consolidation for product data did not settle the concern, because the contract wording was broader than its stated intent.

## What people objected to

- The clause could let Meta scrape publishers' sites and use their journalism to train its AI ([Nieman Lab](https://www.niemanlab.org/2026/10/metas-new-commercial-terms-will-allow-the)).
- Using any Meta business product forces you to accept the scraping rights, with no opt-out (likened to antitrust "tying") ([Nieman Lab](https://www.niemanlab.org/2026/10/metas-new-commercial-terms-will-allow-the)).
- "Test credentials" could get past registration walls or paywalls, and the terms do not commit Meta to honouring robots.txt ([Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/metas-new-terms-expand-crawling-rights-beyond-advertisers/); [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/metas-new-terms-let-it-crawl-business-websites-to-train-ai-from-oct-30/)).
- "Associated with your products or services" is undefined and could reach any linked site, such as articles shared as links ([Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/metas-new-terms-expand-crawling-rights-beyond-advertisers/)).
- Removing the word "train" was cosmetic, since the broad crawling permission stayed ([Relevant Audience](https://www.relevantaudience.com/meta/meta-commercial-terms-delayed-to-november-20-2026/)).

## Who pushed back

News publishers and trade bodies (News/Media Alliance, European Publishers Council, UK News Media Association, Digital Content Next, Norwegian Media Businesses' Association), legal academics, small businesses with Meta accounts, UK and US politicians, and regulators (European Commission, UK CMA, a Norwegian court).

## Did they back down?

Yes, in stages that ended in a full pause: wording softened on 17 October, effective date delayed on 23 October, then the whole update shelved in early November with the old terms kept. Reports differ on whether the pause is permanent, and Meta said only that the update will not take effect on 20 November.
