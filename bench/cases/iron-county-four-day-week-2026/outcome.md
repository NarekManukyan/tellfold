# Outcome: Iron County four-day school week

## Timeline

- Before the vote (date not shown) — A district poll finds 66.6% of about 2,600 parents and 71% of about 760 staff in favour; supporters cite Friday absences and teacher retention ([FOX 13 Utah, staff and parents chime in](https://www.fox13now.com/news/local-news/southern-utah/iron-county-school-staff-parents-chime-in-on-four-day-school-week)).
- 2026-08-25 — Board votes 4-3, after more than four hours, to adopt a four-day week from 2027-28 with an option to delay to 2028-29. Public comment includes people for, against and undecided ([Iron County Today, 26 Aug 2026](https://ironcountytoday.com/?p=13601); [KSL, 26 Aug 2026](https://www.ksl.com/article/51616408)).
- 2026-08-26 — Local TV reports 61% of surveyed parents positive. A working mother says it will cost families childcare money or force a parent to stay home; a parent-educator says it will help about half of families and hurt the rest; a supporter says students need time to recharge. The board vice president, who voted no, says cutting a day will not raise test scores ([KUTV, 26 Aug 2026](https://kutv.com/news/education/iron-county-schools-approve-move-to-four-day-school-week-starting-in-2027)).
- 2026-08-31 — Follow-up coverage: board members describe sleepless nights over students without support at home and over academic risk; one warns parents the district could switch back if the state waiver is not renewed ([Iron County Today, 31 Aug 2026](https://ironcountytoday.com/?p=13626)).

## Overall reaction

Mixed. Surveys showed a clear majority of parents (61–67% depending on the poll) and staff in favour, and supporters spoke up at the meeting. But a sizeable minority, and three of seven board members, opposed it, mainly over childcare and learning time. No organised campaign to reverse it was reported in the weeks after the vote.

## What people objected to

- Friday childcare costs, or a parent having to stay home, especially for dual-income households.
- Lost instruction time and a risk to already low test scores.
- Unequal effects: good for some families, harmful for others.
- Students who lack adult support at home on Fridays.
- Doubts about real cost savings and about extra workload for teachers on longer days.

## Who pushed back

- Working parents and dual-income families.
- Some parents who are also educators.
- Three board members, including the vice president.

Supporters included most surveyed parents and staff, the board member who moved the motion, and families who value longer weekends for activities and rest.

## Did they back down?

No. The decision stands for 2027-28, with the built-in option to delay a year. No reversal or petition was reported as of the sources checked.
