# Iron County School District approves a four-day school week from 2027-28

## Who the district is

Iron County School District serves Cedar City and the surrounding rural county in southern Utah, with nine elementary and eight secondary schools. It ranks near the bottom of Utah districts for per-pupil spending, and recent bond measures to build new schools for a growing population have failed. Several other rural Utah districts, including neighbouring Garfield County and Piute and Wayne counties, already run four-day weeks. Most Iron County schools already finish early on Fridays, between about 12:30 and 1:30 pm.

## How the decision was reached

The district spent about a year studying the idea, with research, town halls and surveys. In a district poll, about two-thirds of parents (roughly 2,600 responded) and 71% of about 760 school employees said they wanted a four-day week. In June 2026 the Utah State Board of Education approved the district's waiver to run fewer school days.

## What was decided

At its meeting on Tuesday 25 August 2026, the Iron County Board of Education voted 4-3 to:

- Move to a four-day school week starting in the 2027-28 school year.
- Allow the district to delay the start to 2028-29 if more preparation is needed (added by amendment).
- Have a committee set academic expectations, with teachers tracking results during the year.

Details still to be worked out include the daily schedule (one option adds about 15–20 minutes to each remaining day to keep instructional time), Friday support for students and families who need it, and ongoing communication with parents. The state waiver lasts three years; if it is not renewed, the district could return to five days.

## Stated reasons

- Student fatigue and stress, and low attendance on Fridays.
- Teacher recruitment and retention.
- Budget pressure in a low-spending district after failed bond votes.
- More family time and fewer clashes with sports and activities.

## How it was communicated

A public board meeting with public comment, followed by a district statement and local media coverage.
