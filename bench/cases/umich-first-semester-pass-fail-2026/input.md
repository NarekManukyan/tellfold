# University of Michigan's main undergraduate college will hide first-semester letter grades from fall 2027

## Who the body is

The University of Michigan is a large public research university in Ann Arbor. Its College of Literature, Science, and the Arts (LSA) is the university's biggest school, with close to 20,000 undergraduates. Like most US universities, Michigan puts letter grades on student transcripts and uses them to calculate grade point averages, which matter for graduate school, jobs, scholarships and honours. A small group of other institutions already do something similar for new students; MIT, for example, has had such a policy for incoming freshmen since 1968.

## What was announced

In early August 2026 the university published details of an LSA pilot programme:

- Freshmen who enter LSA in fall 2027 will have their first-semester final grades shown on external transcripts as Pass (P) or No Credit (NC) rather than as letter grades.
- Those first-semester courses will not count toward a student's overall GPA.
- Students will still receive letter grades and instructor feedback. The university will keep the letter grades internally and use them for academic advising, financial aid and scholarship eligibility, academic awards and athletic eligibility.
- The change applies only to LSA, only to entering freshmen, and only to their first semester. It is described as a pilot.

No vote or formal board decision was announced alongside the policy.

## Stated reasons

- To take some of the pressure off the jump from high school to university and help new students settle in.
- To let students try demanding or unfamiliar subjects in their first term without worrying that an early grade will damage their GPA, and so pursue what the university calls more personally meaningful courses of study.
- To help students "start strong" and to respond to what the university describes as a mental health crisis among college-age people.

## How it was communicated

Through a pilot programme page on the LSA website, from which national news outlets picked up the details. The policy is due to start more than a year after the announcement, in fall 2027.
