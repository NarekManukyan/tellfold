# Outcome: University of Michigan first-semester pass/no credit pilot

## Timeline

- 2026-08-06 — Earliest news report found: LSA will show first-semester grades for fall 2027 freshmen as Pass/No Credit, with letter grades kept internally for financial aid and athletics. The report carries only the university's own statements ([Daily Wire, 6 Aug 2026](https://www.dailywire.com/news/prestigious-college-to-hide-freshmens-first-semester-grades)).
- 2026-08-10 — A higher-education commentary site, republishing Campus Reform material, attacks the policy as shielding students from the difficulty college is meant to prepare them for ([Legal Insurrection, 10 Aug 2026](https://legalinsurrection.com/2026/08/u-michigan-will-hide-grades-for-first-semester-students-to-curb-the-mental-health-crisis/)).
- 2026-08-12 — NPR's national story: some teaching and learning experts worry the change could demotivate freshmen or feed grade inflation, others see it as part of a move toward grading for competency ([NPR via Michigan Public, 12 Aug 2026](https://www.michiganpublic.org/2026-08-12/university-of-michigan-wont-use-letter-grades-for-some-freshmen-next-fall)).
- 2026-08-14 — Sinclair's national desk runs a segment in which Campus Reform's editor-in-chief says Michigan is failing students by not giving them a reality check ([WJLA / Sinclair, 14 Aug 2026](https://wjla.com/news/nation-world/is-new-university-of-michigan-grade-policy-helping-or-hurting-students-hide-first-semester-grades-transcript-campus-reform)).
- 2026-09-05 — On-campus interviews show students split: some back it for stress relief and mental health, others call it "kind of silly", unfair to hard workers, or likely to backfire ([Fox News, 5 Sep 2026](https://www.foxnews.com/media/students-divided-university-michigan-hide-first-semester-grades-2027-freshmen.print)).
- 2026-09-29 (reported) — The university pauses the programme, not cancelling it, to allow "further community input and discussion". President Domenico Grasso says much of the attention consists of "distorted narratives" and that students risk being unfairly portrayed as coddled. Students interviewed remain mixed ([Michigan Public via WCMU, 29 Sep 2026](https://www.wcmu.org/local-regional-news/2026-09-29/u-m-students-react-to-suspension-of-planned-policy-to-hide-letter-grades-of-first-semester-freshmen)).

## Overall reaction

Mixed. Students were divided, with supporters citing stress and mental health and critics citing rigour, fairness and the university's reputation; many had no strong view. Outside the campus, conservative higher-education media were sharply critical, while education experts quoted by NPR were split. The criticism was loud enough that the university paused the pilot about seven weeks after it became public, while defending its intentions.

## What people objected to

- It lowers academic rigour and shields students from the reality of being graded, rather than preparing them for it.
- It could demotivate freshmen and add to grade inflation.
- It is unfair to students who work hard for good grades; grades matter at Michigan and the policy could backfire.
- It could hurt Michigan's standing and rankings compared with peer universities.
- The university would do better to invest directly in mental health and student support than to change grading.

## Who pushed back

- Conservative higher-education commentators (Campus Reform, Legal Insurrection) and an opinion piece in the Wall Street Journal (linked by Michigan Public; not read directly).
- Some Michigan students, especially those worried about rigour, fairness and rankings.
- Some teaching and learning experts concerned about motivation and grade inflation.

Supporters included students who saw it as easing the transition to college and as good for mental health.

## Did they back down?

Partly. By 29 September 2026 the university had paused the pilot for more community input, without cancelling it. The pause came outside the two-week window.
