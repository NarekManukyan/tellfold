# National Transport Authority sets new public transport fares from January 2027

## Who the body is

The National Transport Authority (NTA) is the Irish state agency that plans and funds public transport and sets fares on state-subsidised (Public Service Obligation, PSO) services under the Transport for Ireland (TFI) brand. These include Dublin Bus, Bus Éireann, Irish Rail (including DART and commuter rail), Luas, Go-Ahead Ireland and TFI Local Link. Fares were cut in 2022 and have not had a general increase since 2018. The government provided a record €940 million in PSO funding in 2026. The Transport Minister is Darragh O'Brien.

## What was announced

On 3 September 2026 the NTA published a new fares determination, approved by the Minister, taking effect in January 2027:

- Adult fares rise by about **15% on average** across PSO services, covering cash, Leap card and weekly and monthly tickets. Some individual fares rise by more.
- In Dublin, the TFI 90-minute Leap fare goes from **€2.00 to €2.30**.
- Adult Leap town fares nationwide rise 10% to **€1.65**.
- Intercity rail: express fares up 10%, economy fares up to 20%. Intercity open return tickets are withdrawn.
- Many child fares, especially in regional cities and towns, will **fall**.
- Young adult and student fares stay at a 50% discount.
- Free travel for children under nine and for people aged 66 and over, and other Free Travel Scheme users, is unchanged.
- From 2027 fares will be reviewed every year.

## Stated reasons

- Fares now cover about 38% of operating costs, at the low end of European comparisons; the state pays most of the rest.
- The extra revenue will fund more capacity and new or improved services; the Minister linked it to about 140 new or improved services and to BusConnects and Connecting Ireland.
- The average adult increase is below cumulative inflation of 20.8% since 2022, and fares will still be about 8% lower than in 2018.
- The NTA wants a standard fare structure across the whole TFI network.
- The Taoiseach said the government cannot fund everything indefinitely and that public services need sustainable funding.

## How it was communicated

A press release and published fares determination from the NTA, with interviews by the NTA chief executive and statements from the Minister and the Taoiseach on the day. The national Budget for 2027 is due in early October 2026.
