# Outcome: Irish public transport fare rise for 2027

## Timeline

- 2026-09-03 — NTA announces an average 15% adult fare rise from January 2027; Dublin 90-minute fare to €2.30 ([RTÉ, 3 Sep 2026](https://www.rte.ie/news/ireland/2026/0903/1590193-transport-fares/); [Irish Times, 3 Sep 2026](https://www.irishtimes.com/transport/2026/09/03/bus-and-rail-fares-to-increase-15-on-average/)).
- 2026-09-03 — Commuter groups call the rise "absolutely unacceptable" and say users are paying for the extended fuel excise cut ([RTÉ](https://www.rte.ie/news/ireland/2026/0903/1590193-transport-fares/)). The chair of the Climate Change Advisory Council calls it regrettable because it discourages public transport use ([Irish Times](https://www.irishtimes.com/transport/2026/09/03/bus-and-rail-fares-to-increase-15-on-average/)). SIPTU asks the government to fund a halt, warning it will cost a Dublin commuter about €150 a year and feed into pay claims ([SIPTU, 3 Sep 2026](https://www.siptu.ie/siptu-calls-on-government-to-prevent-15-public-transport-fare-increase/)).
- 2026-09-04 — The Oireachtas transport committee invites the NTA to appear on 16 September to explain the rise; a government-party TD says passengers should not face a done deal. The Minister defends the rise and says he will seek more funding in Budget talks ([RTÉ, 4 Sep 2026](https://www.rte.ie/news/2026/0904/1590365-nta-transport-committee/)).
- 2026-09-10 — Labour, the Greens and People Before Profit councillors and TDs in Dublin condemn the rise as anti-worker and a "slap in the face" for regular users, and call for it to be scrapped ([Dublin People, 10 Sep 2026](https://dublinpeople.com/news/northsidewest/articles/2026/09/10/fury-over-15-fare-hike/)).
- 2026-09-29 — Galway County Council unanimously backs a motion to oppose the rise and meet the Minister ([Galway Bay FM, 29 Sep 2026](https://www.galwaybayfm.ie/galway-bay-fm-news-desk/galway-councillors-to-oppose-public-transport-fares-hike-230863)). Minister O'Brien defends the rise as affordable by EU standards and needed for 140 new services; he does not say it will be stopped in the Budget. The Just Transition Commission is reported to have urged a halt ([Irish Times, 29 Sep 2026](https://www.irishtimes.com/politics/2026/09/29/obrien-defends-bus-and-train-fares-hike-says-public-transport-affordable-by-eu-standards/)).
- 2026-10-06 — Budget 2027 raises PSO subsidy by 11% to over €1 billion but keeps the January fare rise; the Minister expects another fares review for 2028 ([Irish Times, 6 Oct 2026](https://www.irishtimes.com/your-money/2026/10/06/more-than-1-billion-set-aside-for-bus-and-rail-but-passengers-face-another-fare-review/)). The Budget also extends the fuel excise cut and holds active travel funding flat; the Dublin Commuter Coalition and opposition parties criticise it ([TheJournal.ie, 7 Oct 2026](https://www.thejournal.ie/budget-2027-public-transport-7185266-Oct2026/)).

## Overall reaction

Negative. Within days, commuter groups, the largest public-sector union, climate advisers and every opposition party that commented came out against the rise. The common thread was cost of living plus climate: critics set it against the government's fuel duty cuts and asked why public transport users were paying more while drivers paid less. Reported support came only from the NTA and the government. The NTA chief executive said she understood people's frustration.

## What people objected to

- It adds to cost-of-living pressure; workers, students and lower-income commuters hit hardest.
- It is unfair next to the extended cuts to fuel excise for motorists.
- Higher fares push people back to cars and work against climate targets and recent passenger growth.
- It will feed into wage demands across the economy, since commuting is a cost of work.
- The government should fund the NTA to stop it.

## Who pushed back

- Commuter groups, including the Dublin Commuter Coalition.
- SIPTU (trade union).
- Politicians beyond the opposition: a government-party TD on the transport committee questioned the decision.
- Opposition parties: Labour, Green Party, People Before Profit, among others; local councillors, including a unanimous Galway County Council motion.
- State advisory bodies: Climate Change Advisory Council chair, Just Transition Commission.

## Did they back down?

No. The government and NTA kept the increase in Budget 2027 (6 October 2026) and the Minister said another fares review would follow for 2028. As of the sources checked, the rise was still set for January 2027.
