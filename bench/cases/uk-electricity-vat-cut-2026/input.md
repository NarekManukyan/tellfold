# New UK Prime Minister removes VAT from household electricity bills from October

## Who the body is

The UK government. Andy Burnham became Prime Minister in July 2026; John Healey is Chancellor. VAT on domestic energy has been charged at a reduced rate of 5%. Gas and electricity prices for most households are limited by Ofgem's quarterly price cap, which stood at about £1,862 a year for a typical dual-fuel household, and a further change to the cap was due to be set for October.

## Background

Two days earlier, on Sunday 19 July, the new government said it was cancelling the national digital ID programme that the previous administration had planned, a project costed at about £1.8 billion.

## What was announced

On Tuesday 21 July 2026, his second day in office, the Prime Minister announced:

- VAT will be removed from household electricity bills from 1 October 2026, taking the rate from 5% to zero.
- The government puts the saving at about £45 a year for a typical household, based on the current price cap.
- The cost is about £850 million in 2026-27.
- The measure is funded only until 31 March 2027, the end of the financial year. Whether it continues, and any longer-term help with bills, will be decided at the Budget.
- Ministers say the cut is paid for by the money freed by cancelling digital ID.

## Stated reasons

- Immediate help with the cost of living ahead of winter; the Prime Minister described it as taking immediate action to cut taxes on energy bills.
- Ministers said it would give households breathing space and help to bring down inflation.
- The Business Secretary called it a statement of the new government's priorities.

## How it was communicated

A statement and social media post from the Prime Minister, followed by comments from the Chancellor and the Business Secretary in broadcast interviews. It was the new government's first major cost-of-living measure, and the Prime Minister told cabinet ministers to look for other ways to reduce living costs.
