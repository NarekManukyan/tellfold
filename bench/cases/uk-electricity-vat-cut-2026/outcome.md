# Outcome: VAT removed from household electricity bills

## Timeline

- 2026-07-21 — The Prime Minister announces zero VAT on domestic electricity from 1 October, saving about £45 a year for a typical home, funded by cancelling the £1.8bn digital ID programme ([Cyprus Mail / Reuters, 21 Jul 2026](https://cyprus-mail.com/2026/07/21/britains-new-pm-to-cut-taxes-on-electricity-bills)).
- 2026-07-21 — Consumer and industry groups respond: the End Fuel Poverty Coalition calls it a positive statement of intent that does not match the scale of what households face; Uswitch says households will warmly welcome it; Which? calls it positive news but notes the biggest users gain most and relief may be short-lived; Energy UK calls it an encouraging first step; Cornwall Insight says the real question is what the Budget does next ([Irish News / PA, 21 Jul 2026](https://www.irishnews.com/news/uk/energy-vat-cut-does-not-address-scale-of-what-households-are-facing-FABXPZ75QJIIHBWLBDEE3Y75OU/)).
- 2026-07-21 — Funding row: former minister Darren Jones says the digital ID programme was itself unfunded, so it cannot pay for the cut; Conservative leader Kemi Badenoch welcomes action on energy costs but asks how it will be paid for without more tax or borrowing ([LBC, 21 Jul 2026](https://www.lbc.co.uk/news/cost-living/energy-bills-business-secretary-unfunded-tax-cut/)).
- 2026-08-26 — Ofgem raises the price cap by about 4% from October; the regulator and the Energy Secretary say the VAT cut limited the rise ([Irish News / PA, 26 Aug 2026](https://www.irishnews.com/news/uk/winter-energy-price-cap-to-rise-4-TFQTXFDDIFNPRHQ2FG2J32IB24/)).
- 2026-08-27 — Northern Ireland, where EU VAT rules still apply, is set to get a flat one-off payment worth under £5 a month instead ([Irish News, 27 Aug 2026](https://www.irishnews.com/news/northern-ireland/andy-burnhams-flagship-cost-of-living-measure-to-be-worth-less-than-5-a-month-to-ni-households-this-winter-LLQOEPGJTFCVNA6TD6VK4WMJPM/)).
- 2026-10-28 — The Budget extends zero VAT on electricity to July 2027 ([Which?, 28 Oct 2026](https://www.which.co.uk/news/article/what-cost-of-living-support-did-the-government-announce-in-the-budget-a50vz4X02zeY)).

## Overall reaction

Positive, with caveats. Consumer groups, fuel poverty campaigners and energy industry bodies all welcomed the cut, while saying it was small (about £45 a year), temporary, favoured high users and did not match rising bills. The political argument was about funding: critics said the digital ID money it relied on did not exist. No group argued against cutting the tax itself.

## What people objected to

- The saving is small and does not address the scale of energy costs households face.
- It is temporary (funded only to March) and may be short-lived relief.
- Households with the highest bills benefit most; campaigners wanted targeted support and levies moved into general taxation.
- The funding is questionable: the digital ID programme it was meant to replace was itself unfunded.
- Northern Ireland is left out of the VAT change and gets a much smaller substitute payment.

## Who pushed back

- Fuel poverty campaigners (End Fuel Poverty Coalition) and consumer groups (Which?), who wanted more and better-targeted help.
- Political critics of the funding: Darren Jones (Labour, ally of the former PM) and the Conservative leader.

## Did they back down?

No. The cut took effect on 1 October and the Budget extended it to July 2027.
