# Barnet Council cuts paid parking hours outside Mays Lane shops to one hour a day

*Benchmark seed: a neutral summary of the announcement as it stood on 23 July 2026, written for the Tellfold accuracy test. Not the original text.*

## Who the body is

The London Borough of Barnet is the local council for a large outer-London borough. It runs on-street parking, including controlled parking zones (CPZs). Mays Lane in Chipping Barnet (EN5) has a small parade of local shops, including a pet shop, used by nearby residents, many of them older.

## Background

In mid-December 2025 the council replaced free parking in the service road outside the Mays Lane parades with short-stay pay-by-phone bays, charged 8am to 6:30pm, Monday to Saturday. Tariffs are £0.83 for 15 minutes, £1.69 for 30 minutes and £3.38 for an hour. Elsewhere in the surrounding Chipping Barnet "D" controlled parking zone, restrictions apply only for one hour in the middle of the day, which is meant to stop all-day commuter parking without charging short visits. Shopkeepers had told the council the charges were hurting trade, and a petition opposing them had gathered well over 1,000 signatures. The council is also consulting separately on a proposed new Underhill South CPZ nearby.

## What was announced

On 23 July 2026 the council published an experimental traffic order for the short-stay bays outside 92–94, 189–195 and 197–199 Mays Lane:

- From Monday 3 August 2026, the bays will be charged only **2pm to 3pm, Monday to Friday**, matching the rest of the Chipping Barnet "D" zone.
- Outside that hour, parking in the bays is free. Saturday charges end.
- Tariffs during the charged hour do not change.
- The order is an experiment for up to 18 months. Formal comments and objections can be made in writing during the first six months (to early February 2027), after which the council will decide whether to make it permanent.

## Stated reasons

The notice says the change follows feedback received since the pay-by-phone bays were introduced. Aligning the hours with the wider zone is intended to keep discouraging all-day parking while making short shopping visits free.

## How it was communicated

A statutory public notice in the local newspaper and on the council's parking consultation channels, signed by the council's Director of Highways and Transportation, with the order, plan and statement of reasons available on request.
