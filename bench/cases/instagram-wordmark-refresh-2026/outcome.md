# Outcome: Instagram wordmark refresh (announced 2026-08-13)

## Timeline

- 2026-08-13 — Mosseri reveals the new wordmark on Instagram and Threads. His posts draw more than 5,000 combined replies by the time Social Media Today publishes, many of them about the stylised "r" ([Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-reveals-its-new-logo/827871/)).
- 2026-08-13 — Within hours the "Instagzam" joke (the "r" read as a "z") spreads widely. Variety covers the joke itself ([Entrepreneur](https://www.entrepreneur.com/business-news/instagram-just-changed-its-logo-for-the-first-time-in-a-decade); [Creative Boom](https://www.creativeboom.com/news/instagram-reveals-its-first-brand-refresh-in-10-years-with-a-new-wordmark-and-three-typefaces/)).
- 2026-08-13 — A Pollfish survey of 100 US social media users finds 69% prefer the old wordmark and 23% the new one. The new design still gets an average reaction score of 3.8/5, with 65% rating it favourably ([Pollfish](https://ws.pollfish.com/resources/blog/market-research/instagram-logo-wordmark-update-survey-data/)).
- 2026-08-14 — Ars Technica's Aurich Lawson says it looks like AI-generated lettering. Others raise readability and accessibility questions ([Entrepreneur](https://www.entrepreneur.com/business-news/instagram-just-changed-its-logo-for-the-first-time-in-a-decade)).
- 2026-08-14 — Designers are split: some welcome the tidier script, others dislike a mark that is half script, half sans ([Creative Boom](https://www.creativeboom.com/news/instagram-reveals-its-first-brand-refresh-in-10-years-with-a-new-wordmark-and-three-typefaces/)).
- 2026-08-15 — Coverage summarises the reception: some users like it, many are disappointed ([Khaleej Times / ANI](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/business/tech/instagram-unveil-more-modern-wordmark-logo-first-time-since-2016)).
- 2026-08-17 — Instagram's director of product design, Jasmine Probst, defends the design to Variety, saying it is important to "embrace the future" ([ATDb / Variety excerpt](https://www.theatdb.com/news/instagram-head-of-product-design-explains-thinking-behind-that-new-logo-and-responds-to-critics-its-important-for-us-to-embrace-the-future)).
- 2026-08-18 — Boing Boing headline: "Mixed reviews for Instagram's new logo" ([Boing Boing](https://boingboing.net/2026/08/18/mixed-reviews-for-instagrams-new-logo.html), headline only; page could not be fetched).

## Overall reaction

Mixed, and mostly mockery rather than anger. The dominant reaction was jokes about "Instagzam". In head-to-head tests most people preferred the old wordmark, but the new one was not strongly disliked, and many users said they did not care much either way. A social-listening analysis (Pulsar, not opened) reportedly found positive sentiment grew over the first few days. Treat that as unverified.

## What people objected to

- The stylised "r" looks like a "z", so the name reads "Instagzam" ([Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-reveals-its-new-logo/827871/)).
- The lettering is harder to read, which raised accessibility questions ([Entrepreneur](https://www.entrepreneur.com/business-news/instagram-just-changed-its-logo-for-the-first-time-in-a-decade)).
- It looks generic, like AI-generated text ([Entrepreneur](https://www.entrepreneur.com/business-news/instagram-just-changed-its-logo-for-the-first-time-in-a-decade)).
- Instagram should fix the feed (showing posts from accounts people follow) rather than redesign its branding ([Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-reveals-its-new-logo/827871/)).
- It is an awkward hybrid, half script and half sans ([Creative Boom](https://www.creativeboom.com/news/instagram-reveals-its-first-brand-refresh-in-10-years-with-a-new-wordmark-and-three-typefaces/)).

## Who pushed back

Everyday users joking or complaining in Mosseri's replies, designers and design press, and tech writers. There was no organised campaign. Some users and designers liked the change.

## Did they back down?

No. Instagram's design leads defended the design, and the rollout went ahead. No change to the wordmark had been reported.
