# Outcome: carbon tax cut on home heating in Ireland's Budget 2027

## Timeline

- 2026-10-06 — Budget 2027 cuts the carbon tax on home heating oil and gas from €63.50 to €48.50 per tonne and freezes it for the government's term; fuel excise cuts are extended to February ([TheJournal.ie, 6 Oct 2026](https://www.thejournal.ie/carbon-tax-pause-ireland-7183368-Oct2026/); [RTÉ, 6 Oct 2026](https://www.rte.ie/news/2026/1006/1594297-at-a-glance-key-points-from-budget-2027/)).
- 2026-10-06 — Green Party leader Roderic O'Gorman calls the decision "absolutely pathetic", saying people who want to retrofit have been let down and there will be less money in 2027; he notes the SEAI budget rose only about 2% ([RTÉ, 6 Oct 2026](https://www.rte.ie/news/budget-2027/2026/1006/1594236-climate-carbon-tax-budget-2027/)).
- 2026-10-06 — The Greens' finance spokesperson says the government has given up on climate change; Friends of the Irish Environment and Friends of the Earth Ireland question how retrofits and renewables will be funded and call the cut short-sighted; the fuel distributors' body Fuels for Ireland calls it a retreat from coherent climate taxation and says a VAT change would have helped more; the hauliers' association welcomes the excise extension ([TheJournal.ie, 6 Oct 2026](https://www.thejournal.ie/carbon-tax-pause-ireland-7183368-Oct2026/)).
- 2026-10-06 — Opposition parties say it does not go far enough: Aontú says the tax on home heating oil was only cut by 25% rather than removed, and Sinn Féin calls the government's fuel supports cynical, noting fuel allowance recipients end up €12 worse off over the season ([TheJournal.ie FactCheck, 6 Oct 2026](https://www.thejournal.ie/budget-carbon-tax-home-heating-factcheck-government-aontu-pbp-sinn-fein-7184533-Oct2026/)).

## Overall reaction

Mixed. The cut gave households that heat with oil or gas a modest saving and the government framed it as cost-of-living help, but it drew attack from two directions: climate groups, the Greens and even the fuel distributors' trade body said it abandoned a coherent carbon price and threatened retrofit funding, while Sinn Féin and Aontú said it was too small and should have gone further. No organised household campaign either way was found after the Budget.

## What people objected to

- It abandons the agreed carbon tax path and weakens Ireland's climate policy.
- Less carbon tax revenue means less money for retrofits and home energy upgrades.
- It is a short-sighted fix that will not protect families from future fuel price shocks.
- The cut is too small: the tax on home heating should have been removed, and other fuel supports were trimmed.

## Who pushed back

- Environmental groups (Friends of the Earth Ireland, Friends of the Irish Environment) and the Green Party.
- Fuels for Ireland (fuel distributors), on policy coherence.
- Sinn Féin and Aontú, who wanted bigger cuts.

## Did they back down?

No change to the decision was reported in the sources found.
