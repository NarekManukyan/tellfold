# Ireland's Budget 2027 cuts carbon tax on home heating oil and gas and stops further rises for the government's term

## Who the body is

The Government of Ireland, led by Taoiseach Micheál Martin. Budget 2027 was presented to the Dáil by Minister for Finance Simon Harris and Minister for Public Expenditure Jack Chambers. Darragh O'Brien is Minister for Climate and Transport.

## Background

Ireland has charged a carbon tax on home heating oil since 2010. The Finance Act 2020 set annual increases of €7.50 per tonne of CO2 so that all fuels would reach €100 per tonne by 2030. Home heating oil prices had risen sharply in 2026 after the war involving Iran; one price tracker put the average cost of 500 litres at about €808, up over 68% on 2025. More than a third of Irish households heat with oil. After fuel protests in April, the increase due in May 2026 had already been deferred to October.

## What was announced

On Tuesday 6 October 2026, in the Budget, the Government announced:

- The carbon tax rate on kerosene (home heating oil) and natural gas falls from €63.50 to €48.50 per tonne of CO2.
- There will be no further increases on these home heating fuels for the rest of the government's term, about three years.
- The scheduled October increase on petrol and diesel is paused, with rises to resume in 2027, and reduced fuel excise rates are extended to 28 February 2027 before being restored in stages by 30 June.
- Over €650 million in capital funding for SEAI home and community energy upgrades, and about €1.3 billion overall for climate measures including retrofit grants and a boiler scrappage scheme.

A consumer price site estimated the saving at about €45 on a 1,000-litre fill of oil and €30-35 a year for a gas customer.

## Stated reasons

- Heating prices are a heavy burden for many families, and the Government said it must act even though it cannot fully absorb the effect of the Middle East conflict.
- Ministers said the change does not weaken the Government's commitment to decarbonisation, which must be fair, and that retrofit funding will not be cut.

## How it was communicated

The Minister for Finance's Budget speech in the Dáil, Budget documents, and interviews with ministers on Budget day.
