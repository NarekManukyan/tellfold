# Bungie says Destiny 2 will continue and that vaulted campaigns, destinations and raids will return

## Who the body is

Bungie is the American studio behind Halo and the Destiny series, and part of Sony's PlayStation group. Destiny 2 is an online shooter that launched in 2017 and was sold for years as a free base game plus paid expansions. Starting in 2020 Bungie removed ("vaulted") large amounts of older content from the game, including whole campaigns, destinations and raids that many players had paid for, to keep the game's size and upkeep manageable.

## Background

In May 2026 Bungie announced that active development of Destiny 2 would end, with a final content update the following month. Bungie later laid off most of the Destiny 2 team. Its new extraction shooter, Marathon, launched on 5 March 2026 to generally good reviews but drew fewer players than the studio hoped. Destiny 2 has stayed online in a maintenance state since the final update.

## What was announced

On Monday 21 September 2026 Bungie's studio heads, Poria Torkan and Josh Deane, who describe themselves as a new leadership team, published a video and a Bungie.net post called "The Next Chapter for Bungie". They said:

- Bungie is not leaving Destiny and calls it foundational to the studio's future.
- As a first step, it will bring back vaulted Destiny 2 campaigns (Forsaken among them), destinations and raids, for all players.
- The old content no longer works with the current game, so it has to be rebuilt with updated lighting, encounters and enemies. This will take time. No dates were given; Bungie promised progress updates in the coming months.
- Restoring content is meant to lead on to new Destiny stories and possibly new games, without details.
- Marathon did not reach as many players as hoped and will be reworked into a deeper survival game with co-operative PvE, with updates planned for October and December.

## Stated reasons

- The studio heads said Bungie had tried to do too much, had lost sight of its players and had damaged their trust.
- They said vaulted content should never have been taken away and that they want to return Destiny to its original sense of awe, discovery and community.
- They said they were not asking players to trust words alone, that trust has to be earned, and asked players to hold them to account if updates do not arrive.

## How it was communicated

A developer video and a blog post on Bungie.net, shared on Bungie's social channels, followed by press coverage the same day.
