# Outcome: McLaren heritage wordmark

## Timeline

- 2026-09-22 — McLaren reveals the new wordmark and identity; the F1 team posts it on X as "the first chapter of what's next" ([PlanetF1, 22 Sep 2026](https://www.planetf1.com/news/mclaren-new-logo-2026); [autoevolution, 22 Sep 2026](https://www.autoevolution.com/news/mclaren-s-new-wordmark-looks-back-to-bruce-mclaren-as-brand-prepares-to-launch-new-models-275941.html)).
- 2026-09-22 — The Autopian calls it a reasonable effort but says the old logo "was fine" and that McLaren's real problem is a lack of interest in its road cars; reader comments range from "Meh-claren" to praise for the retro grand tourer ([The Autopian, 22 Sep 2026](https://www.theautopian.com/mclaren-changes-its-logo-in-effort-to-make-anyone-care-about-its-road-cars/)).
- 2026-09-22/23 — Creative Bloq headlines that the new logo looks like a luxury fashion brand (search snippet only) ([Creative Bloq](https://www.creativebloq.com/design/logos-icons/mclarens-new-logo-looks-like-a-luxury-fashion-brand)).
- 2026-09-23 — Design Compass reports mixed reactions: some fans say the wordmark weakens the sense of speed and aggression and looks like a fashion or luxury clothing label, that the underlined "c" is hard to notice, and that the old logo was easier to read at a distance; others see a modern restoration of the original workshop sign ([Design Compass, 23 Sep 2026](https://designcompass.org/en/2026/09/23/mclaren-new-logo-controversy/)).
- 2026-09-23 — Supercar Blondie notes the refresh is far less divisive than Jaguar's rebrand and that some may see little difference from the old logo; it says it is unclear whether fans will welcome it ([Supercar Blondie, 23 Sep 2026](https://supercarblondie.com/mclaren-new-logo-founder-racing-heritage/)).

## Overall reaction

Mixed and fairly muted. Commentators and fans split between those who liked the heritage story and clean look and those who felt it lost the brand's sense of speed and looked like a fashion label. Several outlets noted it was subtle and unlikely to cause a major row. No organised opposition and no change by McLaren.

## What people objected to

- The lighter wordmark weakens the sense of speed and aggression.
- It looks like a luxury fashion or clothing brand rather than a car or racing brand.
- The change (small underlined "c") is too subtle to notice; the old logo was fine and easier to read from a distance.
- A logo does not fix McLaren's real problem, which is interest in its road cars.

## Who pushed back

- Some McLaren and F1 fans in online communities.
- Some motoring and design commentators, mildly.

Supporters included fans who valued the link to Bruce McLaren's family workshop.

## Did they back down?

No. The identity is being rolled out as announced.
