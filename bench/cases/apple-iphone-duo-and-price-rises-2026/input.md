# Apple unveils the iPhone Duo foldable and iPhone 18 Pro, and raises prices on its existing iPhones

## Who the body is

Apple designs and sells the iPhone, its biggest product line, along with Apple Watch, AirPods and Mac computers. Its autumn launch event is usually where it introduces the year's new iPhones and sets prices for the whole range. In 2026 its chief executive is John Ternus. Through 2026 memory and storage chips have become much more expensive because of demand from AI data centres, and Apple executives had said earlier in the year that the company could no longer fully absorb those costs. Samsung's Galaxy Z Fold 8 foldable launched in July at $1,899.99.

## What was announced

At an event at its Cupertino headquarters on Wednesday 9 September 2026, Apple announced:

- **iPhone Duo**, its first foldable iPhone, in a book-style design. It starts at $1,999 for 256 GB and goes up to about $3,200 for 2 TB. The unfolded inner screen is Apple's largest iPhone display and the device is its thinnest iPhone when open. It uses Touch ID in the side button rather than Face ID, and has no dedicated telephoto camera. Pre-orders open 16 October and it ships 23 October.
- **iPhone 18 Pro and Pro Max**, from $1,199 and $1,299, each $100 more than the iPhone 17 Pro models they replace. Higher storage tiers rise more (the 1 TB model by $300). Features include a main camera with a variable aperture and longer battery life. Orders open 12 September.
- No new base iPhone 18 at this event.
- **Price rises on existing models:** the iPhone 16, iPhone 17e, iPhone 17 and iPhone Air each go up by $100 (for example, the iPhone 17 to $899 and the iPhone 16 to $799). The iPhone 17 Pro and Pro Max are discontinued.
- Apple Watch Series 12 and Ultra 4, and AirPods 5 at $129 to $149.

## Stated reasons

- Apple presented the Duo as a design "only Apple" could deliver, with an iPad-like hinge and a screen finish meant to minimise the fold.
- Apple did not give a specific reason for the price rises on existing models; they followed months of public comment from the company about rising memory and storage costs.

## How it was communicated

A pre-recorded keynote streamed from Apple Park, press releases and updated prices on Apple's online store the same day.
