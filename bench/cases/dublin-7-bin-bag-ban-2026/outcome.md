# Outcome: Dublin 7 bin-bag ban

*Control case: announced May 2026, before the model's training cutoff. Most of the reaction (June to September 2026) falls after it.*

## Timeline

- 2026-04 (late) — Waste company Greyhound tells Dublin 7 customers they must switch from bags to bins by 6 July ([Dublin Inquirer, 17 Jun 2026](https://dublininquirer.com/revolt-continues-in-dublin-7-over-proposed-shift-from-bags-to-bins/)).
- 2026-05-07 — Irish Times reports the north inner city ban starting the following Monday and the extension to Stoneybatter and Smithfield from July, with no compactors planned there ([Irish Times, 7 May 2026](https://irishtimes.com/ireland/dublin/2026/05/07/bin-bag-ban-hits-dublins-north-inner-city-as-area-seeks-to-dump-dirty-image)).
- 2026-06-15 — More than 300 people attend a public meeting in Stoneybatter hosted by a local Labour TD. Residents say their houses are too small for wheelie bins, bins would sit on the street all week, costs would rise and nobody consulted them ([Dublin Inquirer, 17 Jun 2026](https://dublininquirer.com/revolt-continues-in-dublin-7-over-proposed-shift-from-bags-to-bins/)).
- 2026-06-16 — Council's head of waste management tells the Central Area Committee the changeover is moved to 4 August, with firm enforcement from 4 September; Green councillors say poor communication put the switch at risk ([Dublin Inquirer, 17 Jun 2026](https://dublininquirer.com/revolt-continues-in-dublin-7-over-proposed-shift-from-bags-to-bins/)).
- 2026-07-06 — Dublin 7 residents rally outside City Hall over cost, lack of consultation and lack of space. The council defers the ban to October to allow a public consultation ([Dublin People, 7 Jul 2026](https://dublinpeople.com/news/northsidewest/articles/2026/07/07/residents-rally-against-proposed-dublin-bin-bag-ban/)).
- 2026-09-30 — Council suspends the Dublin 7 ban with no new date. More than three-quarters of consultation respondents are understood to have opposed it. A Green councillor says it cannot go ahead until national law allows a single collector per area. The council still says the derogation must go eventually, and rules out large communal or underground bins for the area ([Irish Times, 30 Sep 2026](https://irishtimes.com/ireland/dublin/2026/09/30/bin-bag-ban-dumped-by-dublin-city-council)).

## Overall reaction

Strongly negative among Dublin 7 residents. Opposition built from a packed public meeting in June to a City Hall rally in July and a consultation in which most respondents were against. Local TDs and councillors from several parties took up the residents' case. The council stuck to its goal of ending bag collection but kept moving the date, then suspended the plan.

## What people objected to

- Small terraced houses have no room to store wheelie bins; bins left out front are an eyesore and would stay on the street all week.
- Higher costs: bin service plans and service fees could cost more than bags; one TD said some households' costs could double.
- No consultation with the community before the decision.
- The private collection market means residents only get the options their waste company offers; critics wanted a single council-run or tendered collector instead.

## Who pushed back

- Residents of Stoneybatter, Smithfield, Phibsborough and nearby streets.
- Local Labour TD Marie Sherlock, who hosted the public meeting and later introduced a bill on single household waste collection.
- Labour councillors in the council's ruling group (credited with the deferral) and Green Party councillors.

## Did they back down?

Yes, in stages. The start moved from July to 4 August (June), then to October with a consultation (July), and on 30 September 2026 the council suspended the ban for Dublin 7 with no date. The council did not abandon the policy in principle: it says the derogation must still be removed and that changes to national waste collection law may be needed first. Scored as **partial** (suspended indefinitely, not formally scrapped).
