# Dublin City Council extends its bin-bag ban to Dublin 7

## Who the body is

Dublin City Council is the local authority for Ireland's capital. Household waste in Dublin is collected by private companies that residents pay directly, not by the council, but the council sets the bylaws on how waste is presented for collection. Bylaws from 2016 require waste to be put out in bins, but more than 900 streets were given exemptions (derogations) to keep using plastic bags because wheelie bins did not suit their properties.

## What was announced

The council is phasing out the bag exemptions area by area:

- Around Temple Bar and Grafton Street (south city centre) bags were banned in late 2025, with large on-street waste compactors installed.
- From Monday 11 May 2026, about 110 streets in the north inner city (Amiens Street to Church Street, the quays to Dorset Street) must stop using bags, with compactors at two locations.
- **From July 2026 the ban extends to Dublin 7**, including Stoneybatter, Smithfield, Grangegorman and Phibsborough. This is a mainly residential area. **No on-street compactors are planned there.**
- Remaining southside residential bag areas follow early in 2027, with the aim of ending bag collection citywide by mid-2027.

In Dublin 7, households will have to switch to containers offered by their private waste company: wheelie bins or smaller caddies. Waste companies have begun writing to customers about the switch, with a July changeover date. Households that keep putting out bags can be fined.

## Stated reasons

- Bags left on the street are torn open by gulls, foxes and pets, and by people looking for deposit-return bottles and cans, causing litter.
- The 2016 bylaws already require bins; the derogations were meant to be temporary.
- The council reports that illegal dumping in the first southside ban area has fallen by about 20 tonnes a month.
- The council's head of waste management says collectors see no need for communal collection and will offer wheelie bins or caddies instead.

## How it was communicated

Through council statements to the press and letters from private waste collectors to their customers.
