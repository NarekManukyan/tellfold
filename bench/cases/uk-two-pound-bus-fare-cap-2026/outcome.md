# Outcome: £2 bus fare cap in England for 2027

## Timeline

- 2026-07-22 — The government announces a £2 cap on single bus fares in England outside London for all of 2027, replacing the £3 cap, with £400m of extra funding taken from international climate finance grants that become loans ([GOV.UK press release, 22 Jul 2026](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/cheaper-travel-for-millions-with-a-third-off-fares); [DfT written statement, 22 Jul 2026](https://www.gov.uk/government/speeches/2-bus-fares-from-january-2027)).
- 2026-07-22 — The Local Government Association calls it a positive cost-of-living measure but asks for a long-term funding model for bus networks; the County Councils Network says it will make a real difference for county residents, wants the cap made permanent, and warns that many rural areas have patchy or disappearing services ([LocalGov, 22 Jul 2026](https://www.localgov.co.uk/Councils-cautiously-welcome-2-bus-fare-cap-/64717); [County Councils Network, 22 Jul 2026](https://www.countycouncilsnetwork.org.uk/news/bus-fares-to-be-capped-at-ps2-in-2027-ccn-responds)).
- 2026-07-22 — Campaign for Better Transport says the cap will make a real difference but that too many people lack a frequent, reliable bus service; a cost-of-living coalition says every pound counts for people who take the bus several times a day. The Chancellor says it costs taxpayers nothing extra because it is funded by savings elsewhere ([Big Issue, 22 Jul 2026](https://www.bigissue.com/news/social-justice/andy-burnham-bus-fares-cost-of-living/); [Campaign for Better Transport, Jul 2026](https://bettertransport.org.uk/media/2026/07/)).
- 2026-07-23 — Development charities object to the funding source: ActionAid UK says turning climate grants into loans penalises countries hit hardest by a crisis they did not cause, and Bond says domestic measures should not come at the expense of development funding. The shadow chancellor welcomes cheaper transport but says the financing has not been explained; opposition politicians also question the scheme's temporary nature ([IBTimes UK, 23 Jul 2026](https://www.ibtimes.co.uk/uk-bus-fare-cap-funding-controversy-1810199)).
- 2026-10-28 — The Budget confirms the £2 cap for 2027 ([Which?, 28 Oct 2026](https://www.which.co.uk/news/article/what-cost-of-living-support-did-the-government-announce-in-the-budget-a50vz4X02zeY)).

## Overall reaction

Positive. Councils, transport campaigners and cost-of-living groups welcomed the lower cap, with caveats: it lasts only one year, cheap fares do not help where buses no longer run, and operators and councils want a long-term funding settlement. The sharpest criticism was about how it is paid for, from development charities (climate grants turned into loans) and from the Conservative opposition (how the government's new pledges will be financed). No group of bus users opposed it.

## What people objected to

- The cap is temporary (one calendar year), with no long-term funding model for bus networks.
- Cheap fares do not help rural areas where services have been cut or have disappeared.
- Paying for it by turning international climate grants into loans harms climate-vulnerable countries.
- The opposition questioned how the government would fund its new commitments.

## Who pushed back

- Development charities (ActionAid UK, Bond) over the climate-finance funding source.
- The Conservative opposition (shadow chancellor, shadow transport secretary) over financing.
- Councils and rural campaigners pushed for more (permanence, service funding) rather than against the cap.

## Did they back down?

No. The cap was confirmed in the 28 October 2026 Budget, to run through 2027.
