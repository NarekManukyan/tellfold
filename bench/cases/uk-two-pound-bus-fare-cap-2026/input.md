# UK government cuts the bus fare cap in England from £3 to £2 for 2027

## Who the body is

The UK government, led by Prime Minister Andy Burnham, who took office in July 2026, with Chancellor John Healey and Transport Secretary Heidi Alexander. The Department for Transport funds a national cap on single bus fares in England outside London; London fares are set separately by Transport for London.

## Background

A national £2 cap on single bus fares began in January 2023 as a three-month offer and was extended several times. The previous administration raised the cap to £3, and a £3 cap is due to run until early 2027. Bus operators that join the scheme are reimbursed by the government for the fare income they lose. Many rural and coastal routes have long distances and, without a cap, some single fares on the most expensive routes could be above £10.

## What was announced

On Wednesday 22 July 2026, in his first week in office, the Prime Minister announced:

- Single bus fares on participating services in England outside London will be capped at £2 from 1 January to 31 December 2027, replacing the £3 cap. The government says this cuts the maximum single fare by up to a third.
- £400 million of extra funding for the cap in England, part of £454 million in total once the devolved governments receive their share through the Barnett formula.
- The money comes from reprioritising the Department for Energy Security and Net Zero budget: some international climate finance will be given as loans to other countries instead of grants.
- Separately, support for six mayoral combined authorities to develop franchised bus networks modelled on Greater Manchester's Bee Network, and a consultation on extra planning powers for mayors over public transport.

The cap covers single fares only and lasts for one calendar year.

## Stated reasons

- Easing the cost of living and helping people reach work, education, healthcare and leisure.
- Helping rural and coastal areas where fares are highest, and young people travelling to work.
- The government cites an estimate that the earlier £2 cap generated about 30 million extra bus journeys in its first ten months.
- The Prime Minister said good, affordable transport links are essential; the Chancellor said the cost would be met from savings elsewhere rather than new taxes.

## How it was communicated

A joint press release from the Prime Minister's Office, HM Treasury and the Department for Transport, a written ministerial statement to Parliament, and comments from the Prime Minister to broadcasters.
