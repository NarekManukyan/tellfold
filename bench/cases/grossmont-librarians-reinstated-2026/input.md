# Grossmont Union High School District agrees to bring back teacher-librarians at its nine high schools

## Who the body is

The Grossmont Union High School District runs high schools in San Diego's East County, with nine campuses. It is governed by an elected board; in 2026 the board president is Robert Shield. Its teachers are represented by the Grossmont Education Association (GEA), whose president, Stephanie Macceca, is herself a teacher-librarian.

## Background

In early 2025 a board majority (Robert Shield, Scott Eckert, Gary Woods and Jim Kelly) voted to cut dozens of student-facing posts, 49 in all, including every teacher-librarian, saying the cuts were needed partly to close a budget deficit. The teacher-librarians had run the school libraries and taught research and technology skills. In November 2025 the GEA filed an unfair practice charge with the California Public Employment Relations Board over the cuts. Several former staff members have also brought civil lawsuits against the district, which are still open.

## What was announced

On Wednesday 21 October 2026 the district and the union announced a settlement of the unfair practice charge:

- The nine teacher-librarians will be offered their jobs back from the 2027-28 school year, one at each campus.
- They return to their previous roles, schedules and stipends, and are protected from layoff for two full school years, through 2029.
- There is no back pay or damages.
- The union withdraws its charge. The district does not admit wrongdoing.
- The agreement covers only the teacher-librarians, not the other positions cut in 2025, and does not settle the separate civil suits.

## Stated reasons

- The board president described the agreement as "focused on the future" and in the best interest of the East County community.
- The district said it settled to avoid further litigation.

## How it was communicated

Announced as a settlement agreement between the district and the teachers' union, with statements from the board president and the union, and reported by local news media.
