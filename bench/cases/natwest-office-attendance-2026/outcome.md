# Outcome: NatWest two-day office minimum

## Timeline

- 2026-10-20 — NatWest tells staff it is scrapping the remote-first category; about 13,500 remote-first staff and 17,300 hybrid staff must come in at least two days a week from February 2027 ([Remote Work Europe, citing BBC, 20 Oct 2026](https://remoteworkeurope.eu/news/2026/natwest-abolishes-remote-first-category-uk-staff/)).
- 2026-10-20 — Unite's general secretary calls it "yet another kick in the teeth" for workers after years of real-terms pay cuts. Unite says the move breaches the consultation process agreed in the 2025 pay deal; its finance officer says homeworking lets many staff manage caring duties and health conditions. NatWest says flexible working remains important and staff will get time to plan ([Reuters via 93.3 The Drive, 20 Oct 2026](https://www.933thedrive.com/2026/10/20/natwest-faces-union-backlash-after-asking-staff-to-spend-at-least-two-days-a-week-in-office/)).
- 2026-10-20 — In Ireland, the Financial Services Union says employers that promoted home working have broken a psychological contract with staff ([Remote Work Europe](https://remoteworkeurope.eu/news/2026/natwest-abolishes-remote-first-category-uk-staff/)).
- 2026-10-21 — Unite demands NatWest pause the rollout, return to the agreed consultation process and negotiate. It launches a member survey on the financial and personal impact and plans online and workplace meetings before deciding next steps. It notes many affected staff are in lower-paid roles ([FStech, 21 Oct 2026](https://fstech.co.uk/fst/Unite_Criticises_NatWest_Over_Tougher_Office_Attendance_Policy.php)).

## Overall reaction

Negative from staff representatives. Both unions covering NatWest staff (Unite in the UK, the FSU for Ulster Bank) condemned the change on the day. The argument was mostly about broken process (the 2025 consultation agreement) and the cost to lower-paid staff, carers and disabled workers, rather than refusing any office time. No staff survey results or petition numbers had been published by the latest source.

## What people objected to

- It breaks the jointly agreed consultation process in the 2025 pay deal; staff were handed a finished plan.
- Higher travel and childcare costs, falling on many lower-paid staff after years of real-terms pay cuts.
- Disruption to caring responsibilities and health arrangements built around long-standing homeworking.
- Particular harm to disabled workers and carers.
- Employers that encouraged remote work are now going back on that understanding.

## Who pushed back

- Unite (UK), at general secretary level.
- The Financial Services Union (Ulster Bank staff in Ireland and Northern Ireland).
- Remote-first staff, many in lower-paid roles, carers and disabled workers, as described by the unions.

## Did they back down?

No, as of the latest sources (21 October 2026). NatWest kept the February 2027 start; Unite's survey and next steps were pending. The window since the announcement is short, so later changes cannot be ruled out.
