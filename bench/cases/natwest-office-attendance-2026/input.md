# NatWest ends its "remote first" category and sets a two-day office minimum

## Who the employer is

NatWest Group is one of the UK's largest banks, with operations including NatWest, Royal Bank of Scotland and Ulster Bank in Northern Ireland and the Republic of Ireland. In 2021 it set up flexible working categories: about a third of staff were classed as "remote first", working mainly from home and coming into an office about two days a month, while others were hybrid. Its 2025 pay agreement with the union Unite included a jointly agreed process for consulting staff and the union on future changes to office attendance. Elsewhere in UK banking, Barclays had recently pushed back a planned move to three office days a week for some UK staff.

## What was announced

On Tuesday 20 October 2026 NatWest told staff that:

- The "remote first" working category will be removed.
- From **February 2027**, staff currently classed as remote first, about **13,497** people, must work from an office at least **two days a week**.
- About **17,272** hybrid workers will also be expected to come in more often than they do now, with a minimum of two days a week.
- In total, more than 30,000 employees across the UK and Ulster Bank are affected.

## Stated reasons

- The bank says flexible working remains an important part of how it works, but it has learned more about the value of time together in person, for colleagues and for customers.
- It says it recognises the change will affect some colleagues and is giving staff time to plan ahead and will support them through the transition.

## How it was communicated

Internal staff communications on 20 October, followed by a spokesperson statement to the press the same day.
