# Outcome: Minneapolis Public Schools drops 2027-28 school closures

## Timeline

- 2026-10-13 — At the board meeting the superintendent recommends no closures or mergers; the board passes a resolution barring closures for 2027-28. Parents in the room say "the fact they are hearing us is very encouraging"; another says good leadership means "admitting this was a mess". Board member Sharon El-Amin calls the superintendent's announcement a "blindside move" but is glad there will be no closures ([FOX 9, 13 Oct 2026](https://fox9.com/news/what-changed-mps-superintendent-reverses-course-closures)).
- 2026-10-14 — The announcement draws cheers and applause; community members and the teachers' union had voiced intense opposition to the proposals. The board chair says "I want this city to heal" ([MPR News, 14 Oct 2026](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2026/10/14/minneapolis-public-schools-backs-off-school-closure-plan)).
- 2026-10-14 — A founder of Minneapolis Families for Public Schools calls it "a victory" and "a sigh of relief", while asking for follow-through: details on enrollment growth, a racial impact analysis, a full financial audit and earlier consultation of families ([MPR News, 14 Oct 2026](https://www.mprnews.org/episode/2026/10/14/minneapolis-reverses-school-closure-plans-parent-calls-for-more-engagement-from-district)).
- 2026-10-14 — Parents feel relief. The Minneapolis Federation of Educators president says she lacks trust in the board's intentions; board member Lori Norvell criticises the two members who drafted the resolution for doing so without clear input from colleagues; reader comments argue closures will eventually be needed given falling enrollment ([Minnesota Daily, 14 Oct 2026](https://mndaily.com/city/minneapolis-parents-feel-relief-as-school-board-votes-against-closing-schools/10/14/2026/eicmndaily-com/)).

## Overall reaction

Positive. Parents, family advocacy groups and the teachers' union had opposed the closure plans, and the decision to drop them was met with applause and relief. The caveats were about trust and follow-through rather than the decision itself: the union leader doubted the board's intentions, parents wanted accountability and real engagement before any future plan, one board member objected to how the resolution was drafted, and some commenters argued that the enrollment and budget problems mean closures are only postponed.

## What people objected to

Little objection to the decision itself. Caveats and concerns raised:

- Distrust of the board and district after the way the plans were handled.
- The process: families should be consulted before proposals are made, and the district should admit the plan was a mess.
- The underlying problems (enrollment decline, budget) are unresolved, so closures may come back.
- Follow-through: parents want a financial audit, an enrollment-growth plan and a racial impact analysis.
- Some board members were blindsided or felt left out of drafting the resolution.

## Who pushed back

No group pushed back against dropping the closures. Mild criticism came from the Minneapolis Federation of Educators' president (distrust), board member Lori Norvell (process), and online commenters who think closures are inevitable.

Supporters included parents, Minneapolis Families for Public Schools, the teachers' union and board members including Collin Beachy and Abdul Abdi.

## Did they back down?

No. The pause on closures for 2027-28 stands as of the sources checked.
