# Minneapolis Public Schools drops its plans to close schools for 2027-28

## Who the body is

Minneapolis Public Schools (MPS) is one of Minnesota's largest school districts, with about 29,000 students in roughly 59 schools, in buildings designed for about 13,000 more. It is run by a nine-member elected school board and Superintendent Lisa Sayles-Adams. Almost half of school-age children in the city attend charter or private schools or public schools outside the district. Five of the nine board seats are on the ballot in November 2026.

## Background

On 15 September 2026 the superintendent presented three "transformation" options to the board. Each would have closed or emptied 15 to 16 school buildings, bringing the district from 59 schools to as few as 44, alongside mergers and boundary changes. The district cited declining enrollment and a budget shortfall of about $40 million, and estimated savings of roughly $635,000 to $705,000 a year. Listening sessions ran from late September to 9 October, and a board vote was planned for 10 November.

## What was announced

At the start of the board's regular meeting on Tuesday 13 October 2026:

- The superintendent recommended that the district not move forward with any school closures or mergers at this time.
- The board then passed a resolution, brought by board members Sharon El-Amin and Greta Callahan, ruling out school closures before the end of the 2027-28 school year.
- The resolution commits district leaders to work on enrollment growth, early childhood enrollment, school start and end times, how students are assigned to schools, and how needs-based funding is shared between schools.

The district's enrollment and budget pressures remain; the board and superintendent said enrollment and the quality of services stay priorities, with the next budget process expected in spring 2027.

## Stated reasons

- The superintendent said the feedback on the proposals had come very clearly, overwhelmingly and from many people.
- She said the community needed more time to take in a change of this size.
- Board members spoke of the need for stability and for the district to take a pause.

## How it was communicated

Announced in person at a public board meeting, followed by the board's vote on the resolution the same evening.
