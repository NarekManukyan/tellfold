# Outcome: The Witcher 3 Remastered as a free upgrade (announced 2026-08-25)

## Timeline

- 2026-08-25 — CDPR reveals the remaster at Gamescom ONL: free for existing owners, out 29 September, on PC, PS5, Xbox Series and Switch 2 ([Engadget](https://engadget.com/2244195/a-remastered-the-witcher-3-will-be-released-september-29); [Game Informer](https://gameinformer.com/2026/08/25/the-witcher-3-wild-hunt-remastered-is-a-free-huge-upgrade-out-september-29); [80 Level](https://80.lv/articles/the-witcher-3-is-getting-a-massive-full-remaster-as-a-free-upgrade-very-soon)).
- 2026-08-26 — Reddit reaction is overwhelmingly positive. Fans who expected to pay $40–50 look for a catch and cannot find one, and one calls it a rebuke to studios doing "cash grabs for remasters" ([Notebookcheck](https://www.notebookcheck.net/The-Witcher-3-remaster-Fans-look-for-a-catch-but-can-t-find-one.1379144.0.html)).
- 2026-08-26 — Fans praise CDPR and say other studios would have charged for the same upgrade ([Tech4Gamers](https://tech4gamers.com/fans-praise-the-witcher-3-remastered/)).
- 2026-08-26 — The practical questions raised: whether saves carry over, whether PC mods break, higher system requirements, Steam Deck performance, and whether the old version will stay available ([Notebookcheck](https://www.notebookcheck.net/The-Witcher-3-remaster-Fans-look-for-a-catch-but-can-t-find-one.1379144.0.html)).
- 2026-09-23/24 — Outside the two-week window: a CDPR side-by-side clip draws criticism on X that the remaster's fire lighting and colours look worse than the original. Subreddit reaction is more mixed, and the clip is labelled work in progress ([Dexerto](https://www.dexerto.com/gaming/witcher-3-fans-slam-remaster-for-looking-worse-than-the-original-in-new-comparison-3412057/)).

## Overall reaction

Positive. Players and press praised the free price above all, set against an industry habit of charging for remasters. It strengthened CDPR's goodwill and built interest in the paid 2027 expansion. The criticism was practical (mods, saves, performance) rather than about the decision itself, and a later wave of visual nitpicking came a month after the announcement.

## What people objected to

- Fear that PC mods will break after the update ([Notebookcheck](https://www.notebookcheck.net/The-Witcher-3-remaster-Fans-look-for-a-catch-but-can-t-find-one.1379144.0.html)).
- Uncertainty over whether existing save files will carry over ([Notebookcheck](https://www.notebookcheck.net/The-Witcher-3-remaster-Fans-look-for-a-catch-but-can-t-find-one.1379144.0.html)).
- Worries about higher system requirements and performance on older GPUs and Steam Deck ([Notebookcheck](https://www.notebookcheck.net/The-Witcher-3-remaster-Fans-look-for-a-catch-but-can-t-find-one.1379144.0.html)).
- Whether the current version will remain available for people who prefer it ([Notebookcheck](https://www.notebookcheck.net/The-Witcher-3-remaster-Fans-look-for-a-catch-but-can-t-find-one.1379144.0.html)).
- Later (late September): some felt the remaster's lighting and colours looked worse than the original ([Dexerto](https://www.dexerto.com/gaming/witcher-3-fans-slam-remaster-for-looking-worse-than-the-original-in-new-comparison-3412057/)).

## Who pushed back

Very little pushback on the decision. Concerns came from PC modders, players with older hardware or Steam Deck, and visual purists who prefer the original look.

## Did they back down?

Not applicable. There was nothing to back down from, and CDPR went ahead as announced. No change to the free-upgrade offer was reported.
