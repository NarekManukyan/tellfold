# Irish government bans e-scooters for under-18s and makes helmets and hi-vis mandatory

## Who the body is

The Government of Ireland, led by Taoiseach Micheál Martin (Fianna Fáil) and Tánaiste Simon Harris (Fine Gael). Transport policy sits with the Minister for Transport, Darragh O'Brien, and Minister of State Seán Canney; enforcement is by An Garda Síochána. Rules for using e-scooters on public roads have applied since 2024, with a minimum rider age of 16.

## Background

In the weeks before the decision, the Garda Commissioner said he would support an outright ban on e-scooters, and the Taoiseach said he was leaning towards a complete ban. Children's Health Ireland and the HSE reported a pattern of serious, life-changing injuries: more than a third of children admitted to Temple Street hospital with a traumatic brain injury in 2026 were hurt in e-scooter incidents, such admissions rose by about 50% in a year, and one or two children a day attend emergency departments with e-scooter injuries. Gardaí had seized 1,412 e-scooters so far in 2026. Some retailers had already stopped selling e-scooters.

## What was announced

On Monday 20 July 2026, after a meeting of senior ministers and the Garda Commissioner at Government Buildings, the Government agreed to:

- Raise the minimum age for using an e-scooter from 16 to 18.
- Make helmets and high-visibility clothing mandatory for all e-scooter riders.
- Bring both measures in from August, with the age change going to Cabinet the following week.
- Legislate later in 2026 to treat e-scooters like other mechanically propelled vehicles, with licensing and registration, and to ban selling e-scooters to minors or selling models that are not legal on public roads.
- Raise the charges for reclaiming a seized e-scooter and give gardaí more legal protection when pursuing e-scooters and e-bikes.

The Government did not adopt an outright ban, but the Taoiseach said that option remains on the table if the new rules do not reduce injuries.

## Stated reasons

- The rise in serious injuries, especially to children.
- Garda concerns that e-scooters are being used in drug dealing and other crime.
- Current rules are hard to enforce because nobody records who buys an e-scooter or how old they are.

## How it was communicated

The Taoiseach spoke to reporters after the meeting; ministers gave further interviews over the following days.
