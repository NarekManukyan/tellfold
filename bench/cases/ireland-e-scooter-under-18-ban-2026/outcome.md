# Outcome: Ireland's under-18 e-scooter ban and helmet rules

## Timeline

- 2026-07-20 — Government agrees to raise the e-scooter age limit to 18, require helmets and hi-vis from August, and legislate for licensing and registration; an outright ban stays on the table ([RTÉ, 20 Jul 2026](https://www.rte.ie/news/ireland/2026/0720/1584131-escooters-ireland-meeting/); [TheJournal.ie, 20 Jul 2026](https://www.thejournal.ie/e-scooter-ban-7108045-Jul2026/)).
- 2026-07-20 — The Dublin Commuter Coalition says raising the age does not address the root cause, licensing could amount to a ban, and helmet and hi-vis rules put a barrier in front of commuters. The chair of the Oireachtas transport committee (Fine Gael) calls it a necessary step but wants an outright ban kept on the table; Fianna Fáil calls it a major step forward; Sinn Féin says nothing will change without resourced policing; People Before Profit says an under-18 ban is the wrong answer for an affordable, green mode of transport ([TheJournal.ie, 20 Jul 2026](https://www.thejournal.ie/dublin-commuter-coalition-says-e-scooter-ban-will-result-in-more-cars-and-more-congestion-7108218-Jul2026/)).
- 2026-07-21 — Sinn Féin leader Mary Lou McDonald says the ban means nothing if not enforced; Labour broadly supports it but stresses enforcement; the Green Party welcomes the decision not to ban outright but wants sales of child-targeted scooters stopped and warns licensing must not become a back-door ban ([RTÉ, 21 Jul 2026](https://www.rte.ie/news/ireland/2026/0720/1584291-escooter-ban-reactions/)).
- 2026-07-22 — The director of LOCO Scooters broadly welcomes the rules and calls the helmet and hi-vis rules commendable, but doubts the age change will stop 12-year-olds and wants parents fined for buying scooters for children ([TheJournal.ie, 22 Jul 2026](https://www.thejournal.ie/ireland-e-scooter-ban-2-7109534-Jul2026/)).
- 2026-09-02 — Helmet and hi-vis rules are signed and take effect that Friday. The age increase is delayed: legal advice says it must first be notified to the European Commission, which can take up to 12 weeks, so the target moves to the end of the year. A hospital consultant says the rules are fine but need enforcement ([RTÉ, 2 Sep 2026](https://www.rte.ie/news/ireland/2026/0902/1590016-e-scooter-age-delay/)).

## Overall reaction

Mixed. Government parties, the Oireachtas transport committee chair, Labour and parts of the retail trade supported the package, and the Greens were relieved there was no outright ban. Commuter campaigners and People Before Profit opposed the under-18 ban and the licensing plan as an effective ban on a cheap, green way to travel, while Sinn Féin, Labour and clinicians said the rules would mean little without Garda enforcement. Others, including the transport committee chair, wanted a full ban kept in reserve.

## What people objected to

- Raising the age to 18 does not tackle the real problem (younger children riding illegally, parents buying scooters).
- Without resourced Garda enforcement the rules will change nothing.
- Licensing, registration and helmet/hi-vis rules amount to an effective ban that will push commuters back into cars.
- Sales of scooters marketed to children should be banned and parents who buy them penalised.
- Some wanted the government to go further, keeping an outright ban firmly on the table.

## Who pushed back

- Commuter and cycling campaigners (Dublin Commuter Coalition).
- Opposition parties: Sinn Féin (enforcement), People Before Profit (against the age ban).
- E-scooter retailers (sceptical of the age limit, against a full ban).
- Supporters of a full ban, including some government TDs.

## Did they back down?

No. The helmet and hi-vis rules took effect in early September as planned. The under-18 rule was delayed from August to a year-end target, but because of an EU notification requirement, not because of the reaction; the policy itself was not changed.
