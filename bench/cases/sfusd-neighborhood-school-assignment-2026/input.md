# San Francisco school board votes to move elementary enrollment from a citywide lottery to neighbourhood assignment

## Who the body is

The San Francisco Unified School District (SFUSD) runs the city's public schools. It is governed by an elected seven-member Board of Education; the superintendent is Maria Su. Since 2011, places in SFUSD elementary schools have been allocated through a citywide choice system: families rank schools anywhere in the city and a lottery with tiebreakers decides who goes where. The system has long been seen as unpredictable and confusing for families.

## Background

During 2026 the district consulted on how to replace the lottery and put forward options, including:

- **Option B:** every child is guaranteed a place at their neighbourhood elementary school, and families may also apply to any other school that has open seats.
- **Option C:** every incoming elementary pupil is automatically assigned to a neighbourhood school. Families who accept are done. They may apply elsewhere only for citywide programmes such as language pathways, K-8 schools and some special education programmes, without a guaranteed seat.

In a district survey of nearly 3,000 respondents, 72% said Option B would meet their needs and 48% approved of Option C. The superintendent recommended Option C.

## What was decided

At its meeting on Tuesday 13 October 2026 the board voted 5-2 to direct staff to develop an elementary enrollment policy based on Option C:

- Staff will draw neighbourhood attendance zones with demographers and set tiebreakers for the citywide programmes.
- A final policy is due for a board vote on 13 April 2027.
- The new system is planned for students starting in fall 2028.

The board took up Option C first; Option B would only have been considered if C failed.

## Stated reasons

- Families "should not have to roll the dice" on where their children go to school; a neighbourhood guarantee is predictable and keeps children close to home.
- The superintendent called Option C the bold choice and said Option B would keep the status quo, leaving schools in the city's southeast under-enrolled.
- Supporters on the board argued that open choice works as an escape valve for the families best able to navigate the system, and that the district should instead aim for a good school in every neighbourhood.

## How it was communicated

A public board meeting with staff presentations and public comment, plus information on the district's school reorganisation web pages.
