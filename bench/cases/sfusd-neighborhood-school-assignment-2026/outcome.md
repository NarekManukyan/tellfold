# Outcome: SFUSD moves elementary enrollment to neighbourhood assignment

## Timeline

- 2026-10-13 — Board votes 5-2 for Option C. Parents at the packed meeting hold signs reading "Listen to parents: Choose Option B"; a middle-school parent asks the board to pause and listen to families. Commissioner Alida Fisher, voting no, says neighbourhood schools will by definition be segregated schools ([Mission Local, 14 Oct 2026](https://missionlocal.org/2026/10/san-francisco-school-board-new-elementary-enrollment-policy/)).
- 2026-10-14 — Coverage of the vote notes the survey (72% said Option B met their needs, 48% approved of C); the SF Parent Coalition's director warns of "big equity issues in the district"; an analysis finds 19 of 58 planned zones short of kindergarten seats ([KQED, 14 Oct 2026](https://www.kqed.org/news/12104230/sfusd-moves-closer-to-neighborhood-based-enrollment-in-historic-vote)).
- 2026-10-15 — Opponents describe "a lot of grief"; the SF Democratic Party chair, a Portola parent, says she would look outside the district if limited to her local school; Commissioner Supryia Ray says the procedure put "a thumb on the scale" for C; the principals' union warns poverty could concentrate in neighbourhood schools. Mayor Daniel Lurie calls the vote an important step toward ending the lottery, and the board vice president and an education philanthropist welcome it ([San Francisco Standard, 15 Oct 2026](https://sfstandard.com/2026/10/15/late-pitch-split-board-grieving-parents-sfusd-s-lottery-vote/)).

## Overall reaction

Mixed. Organised parent groups and many parents who spoke or answered the survey wanted Option B and opposed the loss of choice, especially for lower-income families in the southeast; two of seven board members voted no. On the other side, the mayor, the board majority, the superintendent and some advocates welcomed the end of an unpredictable lottery. Coverage framed the vote as historic and divisive rather than as one-sided.

## What people objected to

- Loss of choice: families wanted a neighbourhood guarantee plus the right to apply to any school with room (Option B).
- Equity: families in under-resourced areas, especially the southeast, could be locked into weaker schools, and neighbourhood schools could become more segregated.
- Families may leave the district rather than accept their assigned school.
- The board ignored its own survey and the parents in the room, and the procedure favoured Option C.
- Poverty could become concentrated in some neighbourhood schools.

## Who pushed back

- SF Parent Coalition (SF Parents) and parents campaigning for Option B.
- Commissioners Alida Fisher and Supryia Ray (the two no votes).
- SF Democratic Party chair Nancy Tung, as a parent.
- The principals' union (UASF) did not take a side but raised the concentration-of-poverty concern.

Supporters included Mayor Daniel Lurie, Superintendent Maria Su, the board majority and education philanthropist Phil Halperin.

## Did they back down?

No. The vote gave staff direction toward a final policy vote planned for April 2027 and a fall 2028 start. No change of course was reported in the two weeks after.
