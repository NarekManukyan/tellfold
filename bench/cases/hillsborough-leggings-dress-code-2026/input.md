# Hillsborough County Public Schools updates student dress code for 2026-27

*Benchmark seed: a neutral summary of the announcement as it stood on 14 July 2026, written for the Tellfold accuracy test. Not the original text.*

## Who the district is

Hillsborough County Public Schools serves Tampa and the surrounding county in Florida and is one of the largest school districts in the United States. Its dress code sits inside the student code of conduct, which the elected school board approves. The 2026-27 school year starts on Monday 10 August 2026.

## Background

During the 2025-26 year, middle and high school students told the board and district staff that the dress code was enforced unevenly: similar clothing was overlooked on one student and penalised on another. In December 2025 a high school senior told the board that this inconsistency undermined the code's own goal of helping students feel safe. District staff then met with groups of middle and high school students to rewrite the rules.

## What was approved

On Tuesday 14 July 2026 the board approved the revised student code of conduct for 2026-27. The dress code changes include:

- Pajamas and slippers are no longer allowed.
- Shorts, skirts and dresses must reach at least mid-thigh when the student is standing.
- Face and head coverings are not allowed, except for religious reasons.
- Jewellery with spikes is not allowed.
- Leggings and spandex may not be worn as outer clothing unless covered by a top or other garment that reaches at least mid-thigh (for example, a long shirt or sweatshirt).

The district also added a statement that the dress code will be enforced in a fair, respectful and consistent way, and will not be applied differently based on body type, size, gender, race, ethnicity, disability or other protected characteristics.

## Stated reasons

- To make the rules clear and apply them the same way at every school.
- The changes came out of conversations between district staff and students who had raised concerns about inconsistent enforcement.
- A board member said consequences should be uniform across students.

## How it was communicated

The changes were adopted at a regular board meeting and published in the 2026-27 student code of conduct. It will be shared with families ahead of the first day of school on 10 August.
