# MBTA announces three fare pilots and extends commuter rail discounts

## Who the body is

The Massachusetts Bay Transportation Authority (MBTA, "the T") runs subway, bus, commuter rail and ferry services in the Boston region. It is a state agency; its general manager, Phillip Eng, reports to the state government led by Governor Maura Healey. During summer 2026 the T ran temporary commuter rail promotions: half-price monthly passes, a $1 weekend companion fare and free "Summer Fridays". Today riders get up to two free transfers within two hours, but only between different modes or different bus routes, and express buses cost up to $4.25 a ride against $1.70 for a local bus.

## What was announced

On Tuesday 28 July 2026 Governor Healey announced:

- **Two-hour unlimited transfers (pilot, from 1 September, six months):** riders pay only the highest single fare for any number of subway, bus and ferry trips within two hours, if they use the same payment method.
- **Express bus at local fare (pilot, from 1 September, six months):** express bus routes drop from up to $4.25 to the $1.70 local fare. Silver Line routes SL1, SL2, SL3 and SLW are excluded. Officials estimate a daily express commuter could save nearly $500 over the pilot.
- **Free fares for car-free seniors (pilot, start date to be announced this fall):** Massachusetts residents aged 75 and over who hold a state ID card instead of a driver's licence would ride bus, subway, ferry and commuter rail free. The T is working with the Registry of Motor Vehicles on eligibility.
- **Commuter rail:** half-price monthly passes and the $1 weekend companion fare are extended through November. Free Summer Fridays are not extended and end in August as planned, because fall ridership is expected to be high.

No cost estimate was given in the announcement.

## Stated reasons

- To make transit more affordable and simpler to use, and to "put money back in people's pockets" (the Governor).
- To encourage riders to keep using the system as ridership recovers.

## How it was communicated

A press release from the Governor's office, with the Governor greeting riders at Belmont commuter rail station and riding into Boston, plus details on the MBTA website.
