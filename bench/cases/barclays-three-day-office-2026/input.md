# Barclays raises minimum office attendance for UK staff

*Benchmark seed: a neutral summary of the announcement as it stood on 17 August 2026, written for the Tellfold accuracy test. Not the original text.*

## Who the employer is

Barclays is a UK-headquartered bank with about 45,000 employees in the UK, working in retail branches, operations, technology and the investment bank. Since the pandemic most office-based staff have worked hybrid. Requirements already differ by business: branch staff have always worked on site, and client-facing investment banking roles are often in the office four or five days a week. For many full-time UK staff in operations and technology, the current minimum is two office days a week. The union Unite represents roughly 36,000 of the bank's UK staff.

## What was announced

In July 2026 Barclays sent country-specific messages to staff setting out new minimum office attendance rules. The details first appeared in the press in mid-August:

- Affected staff must attend the office at least **three days a week** from **October 2026** (5 October).
- Managing directors and other senior leaders must attend at least **four days a week**.
- Roughly half of the bank's 45,000 UK staff are expected to be affected, mostly in operations and technology.
- Staff who already attend three or more days, branch colleagues and roles with higher existing requirements see no change.
- Minimum requirements will keep varying by business area, depending on the nature of the work and operational needs.

## Stated reasons

- Barclays says it recognises the value of flexibility for colleagues but also the importance of working together in person in its offices.
- The extra day for senior leaders is meant to support collaboration, decision-making and leadership visibility.
- The bank says many employees already come in three days or more, so the change brings the rest into line with its wider approach.

## How it was communicated

Through internal staff communications in July, followed by a short spokesperson statement when the press asked about it in August.
